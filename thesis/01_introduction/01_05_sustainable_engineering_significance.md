## 1.5 Sustainable Engineering Significance

The Sustainable Engineering significance of the thesis lies in connecting the state-time consequences of thermal interdependencies to both **resilience** and a **bounded sustainability assessment** of electricity and mobility service.

Resilience is interpreted at the service level: whether required electricity and mobility services can be maintained or recovered as thermal states and thermal interdependencies evolve over time.

Sustainability is assessed through **two pathways**:

```text
Pathway 1 — Direct consequence
Extreme Heat
→ Thermal Interdependency
→ Electricity + Mobility Service Consequence
→ Environmental + Economic + Social Consequence

Pathway 2 — Engineering-response consequence
Improve / Adapt / Restore
→ Changed State / Capability / Service
→ Environmental + Economic + Social Consequence
```

The same three sustainability dimensions are used in both pathways:

```text
Environmental
→ energy use / losses
→ GHG emissions
→ material / resource burden

Economic
→ operating cost
→ maintenance / replacement cost
→ intervention cost
→ service-disruption cost

Social
→ service access
→ service continuity
→ affordability
→ equity
→ safety
```

Only indicators that are relevant to the thermal mechanism or engineering response, measurable within the adopted system boundary, and supported by data are included.

SDG relevance is interpreted only after measurable sustainability indicators are established:

```text
Measured Sustainability Indicator
→ Sustainability Dimension
→ Relevant SDG Target / Indicator
```

The thesis therefore does not equate resilience with sustainability:

```text
Resilience improvement ≠ sustainability improvement
```

A response can improve service resilience while increasing energy use, emissions, cost, material burden, or unequal service consequences. Conversely, a sustainability benefit alone does not establish resilience.

The Sustainable Engineering logic is therefore:

```text
thermal mechanism
→ state-time service consequence
→ resilience interpretation
→ direct sustainability consequence
and/or
→ engineering response
→ response sustainability consequence
```

This is a bounded assessment framework, not a full life-cycle assessment, economy-wide analysis, or universal sustainability optimization.
