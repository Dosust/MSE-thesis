# 1.8 General Research Objective

The research problem, bounded gaps, system definition, and seven-stage architecture established in Sections 1.1–1.7 converge on a single overarching research destination. The **general objective** of this thesis is:

> **To develop and validate an evidence-grounded mathematical and engineering framework for coupled Power–Transportation infrastructure that links multilayer structure, causal interdependencies, hybrid dynamics, information and organizational control, controlled viability, intervention effectiveness and efficiency, sustainability requirements, and infrastructure transformation under uncertainty.**

This objective defines the intended **scientific capability** of the thesis rather than prescribing a particular computational implementation. In this context, *develop* means to construct a traceable framework in which system structure, accepted causal mechanisms, coupled dynamics, feasible knowledge and action, resilience assessment, and engineering intervention consequences are connected consistently; *validate* means to subject the corresponding scientific claims to the evidence, verification, validation, uncertainty, comparator, and acceptance requirements defined by the research design.

The objective is bounded to the coupled **Power–Transportation physical system**, with **Information and Organization** represented as supporting domains that influence observation, interpretation, coordination, authority, resources, and feasible control. It does not imply universal treatment of all infrastructure sectors, hazards, organizational arrangements, or sustainability dimensions. Likewise, **controlled viability** is not interpreted as viability alone; within the canonical framework it belongs to the broader resilience assessment of whether critical services can **remain acceptable, recover when degraded, and do so with quantified confidence under uncertainty**.

Accordingly, achievement of the general objective requires the thesis to progress from representation and causal qualification to dynamic analysis, resilience diagnosis, intervention evaluation, sustainability requirements, and defensible engineering transformation. The overarching research question (RQ_0) therefore provides the interrogative counterpart of this objective, while (RQ_1)–(RQ_7) and (SO_1)–(SO_7) operationalize it into specific scientific questions and required accomplishments.

The relationship is therefore:

```math
\boxed{
GO \Longleftrightarrow RQ_0
}
```

at the overarching level, and

```math
\boxed{
SO_k \Longleftrightarrow RQ_k,
\qquad k=1,\ldots,7
}
```

at the specific level. Hypotheses are introduced only where a predicted comparative outcome is scientifically testable; consequently, the seven research questions do not require seven hypotheses.
