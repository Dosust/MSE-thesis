# 1.12 Proposed Contributions

The contributions of this thesis are stated as **candidate scientific and engineering contributions**. They are not treated as established results or as claims of novelty before the corresponding evidence, validation, and novelty gates close. A candidate contribution may be promoted to a supported contribution only within the validity domain justified by the completed research.

## C1 — Evidence-qualified P–T–I–O representation

The thesis is intended to contribute a bounded representation of coupled **Power–Transportation** infrastructure that distinguishes the physical \(P+T\) core from the supporting Information and Organization domains while keeping system boundaries, interfaces, states, service requirements, and admissible interactions explicit. This contribution depends on successful RQ1/SO1 qualification.

## C2 — Causal dynamic Power–Transportation interdependency representation

The thesis is intended to contribute an evidence-grounded representation that distinguishes common exposure from causal dependency and links qualified directional mechanisms to spatiotemporal coupled dynamics. This contribution is conditional on the RQ2–RQ3/SO2–SO3 chain and the evidence obtained under H1.

## C3 — Uncertainty-aware viability–reachability resilience assessment

If supported by H3, the thesis is intended to contribute a resilience assessment that augments conventional performance/recovery curves with prospective information on whether required coupled services can **remain acceptable**, **recover acceptably**, and do so with declared **probability/confidence or robustness** under feasible controls.

## C4 — Explicit information, organization, and feasible-control effects

If supported by H2, the thesis is intended to contribute an explicit representation of how observation quality, information availability, organizational conditions, authority, coordination, and feasible control affect coupled-system response and recovery. Information and Organization remain supporting domains rather than additional physical infrastructure systems.

## C5 — Intervention effectiveness, efficiency, and sustainability-consequence evaluation

The thesis is intended to contribute an engineering evaluation that preserves

\`\`\`math
\boxed{
\text{effectiveness}
\neq
\text{resource efficiency}
\neq
\text{sustainability consequence}.
}
\`\`\`

If the Stage-6 admission and H4 gates close successfully, resilience improvement will be linked to admitted consequence quantities such as qualified cost, operational energy, carbon/GHG, accessibility, or other approved metrics within declared accounting boundaries. Conditional metrics are not promoted to confirmed contributions before their data and VVUQ gates pass.

## C6 — Joint resilience–sustainability engineering decision support under uncertainty

If supported by H5, the thesis is intended to contribute a decision formulation that compares resilience-only engineering choices with decisions that also account for admitted sustainability consequences, feasibility, and uncertainty. Adaptive or sequential transformation remains a supporting Stage-7 extension unless separately activated and validated.

The contribution traceability is

\`\`\`math
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
C_1 &\leftarrow RQ_1/SO_1,\\
C_2 &\leftarrow RQ_2\text{--}RQ_3/SO_2\text{--}SO_3/H_1,\\
C_3 &\leftarrow RQ_5/SO_5/H_3,\\
C_4 &\leftarrow RQ_4/SO_4/H_2,\\
C_5 &\leftarrow RQ_6/SO_6/H_4,\\
C_6 &\leftarrow RQ_7/SO_7/H_5.
\end{aligned}
}
\`\`\`

A final contribution claim requires the corresponding chain to close:

\`\`\`math
\boxed{
\text{qualified construct}
\rightarrow
\text{test/evidence}
\rightarrow
\text{VVUQ}
\rightarrow
\text{bounded result}
\rightarrow
\text{supported contribution}.
}
\`\`\`

Novelty is evaluated separately. A supported contribution is not automatically novel, and literature silence is not evidence of novelty.
