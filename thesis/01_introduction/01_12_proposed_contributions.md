# 1.12 Proposed Contributions

The contributions of this thesis are stated at the proposal stage as **candidate scientific and engineering contributions**. They are not treated as established results, validated advances, or claims of novelty before the corresponding evidence, verification, validation, uncertainty, and novelty gates close. Their role in Chapter I is therefore to specify what the research is intended to contribute **if the associated research questions and hypothesis tests are successfully resolved within the declared validity domain**.

The proposed contribution set is:

## C1 — Evidence-qualified P–T–I–O representation

The thesis is intended to contribute a bounded representation of coupled **Power–Transportation** infrastructure in which the physical (P+T) system is distinguished from the supporting **Information** and **Organization** domains, while spatial location, service requirements, interfaces, states, and admissible interactions remain explicit.

This contribution is realized only if RQ1/SO1 passes its structural, provenance, topology, and boundary-consistency obligations.

## C2 — Causal dynamic Power–Transportation interdependency representation

The thesis is intended to contribute an evidence-grounded representation that distinguishes common exposure from causal dependency and connects qualified directional mechanisms to spatiotemporal coupled dynamics. Its scientific value depends on whether the resulting dynamic causal representation provides useful information beyond independent and static-coupling alternatives.

This contribution is therefore conditional on the RQ1–RQ3 chain and, in particular, on the evidence obtained under H1.

## C3 — Uncertainty-aware viability–reachability resilience assessment

If supported by H3, the thesis is intended to contribute a resilience assessment that supplements conventional performance/recovery curves with prospective information about whether required coupled services can **remain acceptable**, **recover to an acceptable region**, and do so with declared **probability/confidence or robustness** under feasible controls.

The contribution claim is limited to the tested domain and is made only if the viability–reachability object provides preregistered incremental predictive or decision-relevant information beyond the conventional benchmark.

## C4 — Explicit information, organization, and feasible-control effects

If supported by H2, the thesis is intended to contribute an explicit representation of how observation quality, information availability, organizational conditions, authority, coordination, and feasible control affect response timing, service trajectories, and recovery in the coupled system.

This contribution is not a claim that Information or Organization constitute additional physical infrastructure networks. It is limited to the supporting-domain mechanisms and variables that are evidenced, parameterized, and tested within the study.

If the Stage-6 admission and H4 gates close successfully, the contribution would consist of linking resilience improvement to admitted intervention consequences such as qualified cost, operational energy, carbon/GHG, accessibility, or other approved quantities within declared accounting boundaries. No sustainability quantity is promoted to a confirmed contribution while it remains conditional or lacks sufficient data and VVUQ support.

## C6 — Joint resilience–sustainability engineering decision support under uncertainty

If supported by H5, the thesis is intended to contribute a decision formulation that compares resilience-only engineering choices with decisions that also account for admitted sustainability consequences, feasibility, and uncertainty.

The contribution would be established only if the joint evaluation materially changes the admissible/nondominated intervention set, dominance structure, or decision regret under the preregistered decision rule. Adaptive or sequential transformation remains a supporting Stage-7 extension unless separately activated and validated.
