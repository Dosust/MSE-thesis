## 1.12 Proposed Contributions

**State-time authority:** `project/CANONICAL_STATE_TIME_DEFINITIONS_V1.md`  
**Sustainability authority:** `project/CANONICAL_SUSTAINABILITY_TWO_PATHWAY_ASSESSMENT_V1.md`  
**Effectiveness / energy-efficiency authority:** `project/CANONICAL_EFFECTIVENESS_ENERGY_EFFICIENCY_PLACEMENT_V1.md`

The proposed contributions are bounded by the thermal-only scope and remain conditional until supported by completed evidence and validation.

### C1 — Thermal-interdependency qualification

A reproducible distinction between common heat exposure and a directional thermal Power–Electrified Transportation interdependency, including mechanism, thermal state/capability, direction, and validity conditions.

### C2 — Physics-based state-time representation

A minimum-sufficient physics representation linking admitted thermally active Power states, admitted Electrified-Transportation states, and cross-system coupling quantities through time, using the canonical distinction between external forcing, state, capability, coupling, and service.

### C3 — Timing and service-consequence characterization

A quantified characterization of critical thermal transition time, interdependency propagation delay, and resulting electricity and mobility service trajectories where these quantities are identifiable.

State-time contribution boundary:

```text
external forcing ≠ state ≠ capability ≠ service
transition time ≠ propagation delay ≠ service-consequence time ≠ recovery time
```

The exact active state set remains open until independently qualified; the contribution is not the inclusion of more states, but the minimum-sufficient state-time representation required to explain the qualified thermal interdependency and service consequence.

The contribution chain is:

```text
Qualify thermal mechanism
→ Model state-time evolution
→ Quantify service consequence
```

The thesis does not claim contribution from broad Information/Organization modeling, generic viability theory, comprehensive sustainability optimization, or universal infrastructure decision frameworks.


### Supporting sustainability assessment

Where data support it, the quantified service consequences and any engineering responses are interpreted through the canonical two-pathway sustainability framework:

```text
Pathway 1: Thermal Interdependency → Service Consequence → Environmental / Economic / Social Consequence
Pathway 2: Engineering Response → Changed Service → Environmental / Economic / Social Consequence
```

This is a bounded assessment output, not a fourth primary objective or a standalone novelty claim. SDG relevance is linked only from measured sustainability indicators to relevant SDG targets or indicators.

SDG interpretation follows only this chain:

```text
Measured Sustainability Indicator
→ Sustainability Dimension
→ Relevant SDG Target / Indicator
```


### Effectiveness and energy-efficiency interpretation

Effectiveness, where an engineering response is tested, is interpreted within the resilience / engineering-response layer. Energy efficiency, where relevant and measurable, is interpreted within environmental sustainability.

```text
Effectiveness ≠ Energy Efficiency ≠ Sustainability
Effective Resilience Response ≠ Sustainable Response
```

These placements are interpretation rules, not separate novelty claims or additional primary objectives.
