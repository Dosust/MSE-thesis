# 1.13 Thesis Organization

The thesis is organized as a seven-chapter argument in which each chapter has a distinct scientific responsibility. The document structure is aligned with the research contract established in Chapter 1 and with the governing principle that evidence supports models, models support experiments, experiments produce bounded results, and only then may conclusions and contributions be closed.

```math
oxed{
	ext{Research Contract}
ightarrow
	ext{Evidence/Theory}
ightarrow
	ext{Framework}
ightarrow
	ext{Methods/Tests}
ightarrow
	ext{Results}
ightarrow
	ext{Interpretation}
ightarrow
	ext{Closure}.
}
```

## Chapter 1 — Introduction: research contract

Chapter 1 defines the problem to be solved and the limits within which the thesis may make claims. It establishes the engineering context, coupled Power–Transportation system boundary, research problem, evidence-qualified gaps, sustainable-engineering significance, scope and assumptions, seven-stage research architecture, general objective, RQ0/RQ1–RQ7, SO1–SO7, H1–H5, and proposed contributions. Its output is the **research contract** against which the remainder of the thesis is evaluated.

## Chapter 2 — Literature Review and Theoretical Foundations: evidence and theory

Chapter 2 establishes the evidence and theoretical foundations needed to support the research architecture. It synthesizes infrastructure interdependency, coupled Power–Transportation mechanisms, resilience theory and metrics, viability and reachability, observation/information/organization/control, sustainable-engineering consequences, and decision-support concepts. It also separates established evidence from proposed thesis constructs and supplies the evidence base required to qualify mechanisms, assumptions, comparators, and gap claims. Its output is an **evidence-qualified theoretical foundation** for the mathematical framework and experiments.

## Chapter 3 — Conceptual and Mathematical Framework: formal scientific representation

Chapter 3 translates the bounded research problem into formal scientific objects. It defines the system ontology, multilayer graph representation, directional interdependencies, hybrid multiscale dynamics, observation/state-estimation and feasible-control structure, uncertainty-aware viability/reachability resilience object, intervention-consequence mapping, and engineering-decision formulation. The seven-stage architecture introduced conceptually in Chapter 1 is formalized here with explicit definitions and handoffs. Its output is the **mathematical framework to be tested**, not evidence that the framework is already valid.

## Chapter 4 — Methodology and Experimental Design: tests, VVUQ, and falsification

Chapter 4 specifies how the Chapter-3 constructs and Chapter-1 hypotheses are tested. It defines data/evidence qualification, case admissibility, baselines and comparators, held constants, treatments, scenario construction, experiment design, verification, validation, uncertainty quantification, sensitivity analysis, practical-significance criteria, preregistration controls, and falsification/non-support rules. The chapter operationalizes H1–H5 without changing their meaning after results are observed. Its output is a **reproducible and auditable experimental protocol**.

## Chapter 5 — Results: bounded experimental evidence

Chapter 5 reports the outputs of the declared experiments and analyses. Results are organized by the preregistered RQ/SO/H structure and are presented with their uncertainty, sensitivity, comparator, and decision criteria. This chapter distinguishes numerical/model output from interpretation and does not promote a result beyond the validation domain established in Chapter 4. Its output is the **bounded empirical/computational evidence** required for scientific adjudication.

## Chapter 6 — Discussion: interpretation and sustainable-engineering implications

Chapter 6 interprets the Chapter-5 evidence in relation to the mechanisms, assumptions, competing explanations, literature, and validity limits established earlier. It evaluates what the findings imply for coupled-infrastructure resilience, intervention effectiveness, resource efficiency, sustainability consequences, and engineering decision-making without exceeding the evidence domain. It also examines assumption sensitivity, failure cases, limitations, transferability, and unresolved uncertainties. Its output is the **bounded scientific and engineering interpretation** of the results.

## Chapter 7 — Conclusions and Future Work: RQ/hypothesis closure

Chapter 7 closes the research contract. It answers RQ0 and RQ1–RQ7 at the strength justified by the evidence, records the status of H1–H5, identifies which proposed contributions are actually supported, states the final validity domain and limitations, and separates resolved findings from unresolved scientific and engineering questions. Future work is derived from the remaining evidence gaps, blocked tests, model limitations, and unclosed contribution claims rather than from unsupported expansion of scope. Its output is the **final research closure and bounded contribution statement**.
