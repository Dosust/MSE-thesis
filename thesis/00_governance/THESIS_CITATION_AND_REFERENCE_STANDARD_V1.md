## 0A. FROZEN USER REQUIREMENT — VISIBLE IN-TEXT CITATIONS IN ALL THESIS CONTENT (2026-10-08)

**Status: MANDATORY, THESIS-WIDE, ALL OUTPUT FORMATS.** This rule clarifies the existing standard and takes precedence over older advice in this file allowing source-dependent claims to be left without a visible citation in thesis-facing slides, explanations, answers, figures, chapter drafts, and other deliverables. It does **not** change the current thesis title, physical scope, frozen objectives, or externally established science.

**Absolute writing workflow rule:** NEVER deliver substantive thesis-related scientific content with uncited externally sourced claims, even when the content is concise, a presentation outline, a slide bullet, an explanation, a table, a diagram, or a mathematical formulation. A reference list at the end of a response/deck/chapter is insufficient without explicit citations adjacent to the supported claims. Do not use citations as decoration: they must trace to evidence that supports the **exact** claim and validity domain.

### What citations look like

1. **Scientific external claims:** Attach an accurate, verified APA 7 author–date citation immediately after or within the supported sentence or bullet, e.g. `(Verified Author et al., YYYY)` **only when the actual source metadata and claim support have been verified**. Never print a template or illustrative surname as a real citation. A directly verified standard may appear as an organization/standard identifier with edition/year and verified clauses when required.
2. **Frozen thesis-specific definitions/decisions:** Reproduce the official frozen wording exactly; attribute it locally to its exact canonical repository file, section, and version where a source reference is useful (for example, a slide/source note or an in-text `(Thesis canonical scope definition, V1, §1)`). Internal provenance is NOT an external peer-reviewed scientific source.
3. **Original thesis content:** Clearly mark proposed research questions, internal decisions, hypotheses, assumptions, model candidates, derived equations, and intended contributions as *proposed*, *thesis-defined*, *T1*, or *OPEN* as appropriate. Cite the external physical/methodological foundations in the surrounding explanation, and cite the internal authority if the item is governed by a frozen file. **Do not falsely attribute original aims or equations to literature.**
4. **Unavailable or unverified evidence:** Mark the factual assertion `[OPEN — source verification required]` and do not phrase it as established. If the request is to produce a final scientific presentation/chapter and evidence is unavailable, omit or explicitly flag the unsupported claim; NEVER invent a reference to meet a citation quota.
5. **Every output medium:** In academic prose use adjacent author–date citations with a verified reference list; in slides place short legible author–date citations in the same bullet/claim or an unambiguous same-slide source note; in tables cite the source in the relevant cell/row or explanatory line; in figures cite sources and label thesis-derived elements in the caption; in math place provenance immediately around equations; in conversational thesis analysis use inline linked references/citations adjacent to external claims where supported.
6. **Prior art, gaps, and novelty:** Cite the closest established work **and** the exact limitation being contrasted; never claim an entire topic unstudied based on a limited search. A proposed integration is not a proven novelty or a validated contribution.
7. **No citation laundering:** Search snippets, secondary summaries, generic reviews, unmatched citations, and bibliography-only sources cannot substantiate unverified physical laws, equipment parameters, manufacturer thresholds, or validation. Every source reference must support the claimed device, direction, context, methods, and validity domain.
8. **Citation QA gate before release:** Audit every paragraph/slide/bullet/equation/figure/table for any uncited substantive external claim; check adjacent placement, source verification, claim/source alignment, in-text-to-reference-list matching, and visibility. If verification fails, label `CITATION BLOCKED` / `OPEN` and withhold the unsupported claim, not the whole user response.

### Interpretation and precedence

The existing Section 4 rule against padding original thesis objectives and self-defined constructs with irrelevant external references **remains valid**; however, every thesis-related response must make its evidence provenance visible where evidence is asserted. An entire thesis-facing deliverable may not rely only on a bibliography. The older Chapter-I section numbering/content mapped in Section 15 is **historical** where it conflicts with the current 11-section Chapter 1 outline and frozen October 2026 extreme-heat scope; do not revive the older RQ1–RQ7, SO1–SO7, P+T+I+O, or generic resilience scope.

Do not silently weaken this requirement. Changes to the rule require explicit user authorization and a formally recorded, versioned repository governance change; ASU/committee's controlling formatting rules retain precedence.

## 1. Purpose

This standard governs how sources are cited, referenced, verified, and connected to scientific claims across the thesis.

The central rule is:

```math
\boxed{
\text{correct citation format}
\neq
\text{adequate scientific evidence}
}
```

APA 7 controls **how** an accepted source is cited. The thesis evidence architecture controls **whether** that source is admissible for the exact claim.

## 3. What requires an in-text citation

Use an adjacent citation for externally sourced statements about:

- established scientific facts or mechanisms;
- definitions adopted from literature;
- what prior studies found, modeled, measured, compared, or concluded;
- literature-wide capabilities, limitations, trends, or gaps;
- external equations, algorithms, thresholds, parameters, datasets, standards, and taxonomies;
- real-world data, events, policies, or institutional requirements;
- claims that a method or construct is established in a field.

A substantive external claim is not considered evidence-qualified merely because a citation is present. The source must support the exact proposition, system/population, mechanism, method, and validity domain asserted.

## 4. What normally does not require an external citation

Do not add citations merely for appearance to:

- thesis-defined scope and exclusions;
- RQ0/RQ1–RQ7;
- SO1–SO7;
- H1–H5;
- declared assumptions;
- thesis-derived mathematical objects;
- proposed framework stages;
- proposed contributions;
- internal comparator definitions such as `M_0,M_1,M_2`;
- internal PASS/FAIL/BLOCKED rules;
- synthesis statements that are explicitly identified as thesis synthesis.

When a thesis construct is inspired by literature, cite the external foundations in the surrounding explanation and explicitly state that the integrated construct is a **thesis synthesis** rather than attributing the whole construct to one source.

## 5. Citation placement rule

Citations must be **adjacent to the claim they support**.

Preferred:

> Dynamic P–T models already exist for traffic–power resilience and charging interactions (Fotouhi et al., 2017; Wang et al., 2026).

Avoid placing one citation at the end of a long paragraph if it is unclear which claims it supports.

One citation may support several consecutive sentences only when:
1. the source clearly supports all of them; and
2. the attribution remains unambiguous.

## 6. APA 7 in-text forms

### One author

Parenthetical:
```text
(Ouyang, 2014)
```

Narrative:
```text
Ouyang (2014)
```

### Two authors

Parenthetical:
```text
(Poulin & Kane, 2021)
```

Narrative:
```text
Poulin and Kane (2021)
```

### Three or more authors

Use `et al.` from the first citation:

```text
(Rinaldi et al., 2001)
```

or

```text
Rinaldi et al. (2001)
```

### Multiple sources for one claim

Place sources in one parenthesis, separated by semicolons, normally alphabetized by first-author surname:

```text
(Béné & Doyen, 2018; Poulin & Kane, 2021; Vinod & Oishi, 2021)
```

### Direct quotation

Prefer paraphrase in the thesis. When exact wording is necessary, include author, year, and page number where available:

```text
(Author, 2024, p. 15)
```

For a source without pages, follow APA 7 locator guidance using a paragraph number, section heading, or other valid locator when needed.

## 7. Citation strength and verbs

Use verbs consistent with the evidence:

**External evidence**
- reports
- documents
- identifies
- finds
- models
- demonstrates within the study domain
- supports

**Thesis construct**
- defines
- represents
- proposes
- formulates

**Hypothesis**
- tests
- evaluates
- hypothesizes

**Conditional statement**
- may
- can under declared conditions
- is considered where qualified
- remains subject to validation

Avoid `proves`, `guarantees`, `isolates`, `optimal`, or `universally demonstrates` unless the evidence and mathematics explicitly warrant those terms.

## 8. Claim-to-source admissibility check

Before retaining a citation, verify:

```math
\boxed{
\text{claim}
\rightarrow
\text{source identity}
\rightarrow
\text{source type}
\rightarrow
\text{mechanism / proposition supported}
\rightarrow
\text{domain}
\rightarrow
\text{limitations}
}
```

A source passes only if it supports the actual wording used.

For each important claim, ask:
1. Is the source primary, review, standards-based, or secondary?
2. Does it address the same infrastructure system or a defensibly transferable one?
3. Does it support the mechanism or only the general topic?
4. Is the claim causal, descriptive, mathematical, empirical, or normative?
5. Does the source support that claim type?
6. Are there material limitations or contradictory evidence that must be acknowledged?

## 9. Evidence roles

Each citation should have a clear role:

| Role | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Definition/theory | establishes accepted concept or mathematical foundation |
| Mechanism evidence | supports a physical/causal dependency |
| Method legitimacy | supports use of a method, model family, metric, or comparator |
| Empirical evidence | supports an observed relationship or measured effect |
| Gap evidence | supports what literature already covers and what remains unresolved |
| Standard/requirement | supports an institutional, engineering, or reporting requirement |
| Data provenance | supports dataset identity, vintage, units, completeness, or source authority |

A source may serve several roles, but the role must be traceable.

## 10. Research-gap citation rule

A gap paragraph must not be written as `no one has done X` unless a reproducible novelty search supports that statement.

Preferred structure:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Established capability}
\rightarrow
\text{documented limitation}
\rightarrow
\text{bounded unresolved handoff}
\rightarrow
\text{thesis test}
}
```

Citations belong mainly on the **established capability** and **documented limitation**. The final handoff and thesis test are thesis synthesis unless directly adopted from literature.

## 11. Equation and mathematical attribution

For an externally adopted or adapted equation:
- cite the source next to the equation or in the immediately preceding/following sentence;
- state whether it is adopted, adapted, generalized, or re-derived;
- do not cite a paper merely because it uses vaguely similar mathematics.

For a thesis-derived equation:
- identify its assumptions and derivation route;
- do not add a literature citation unless a component is externally adopted.

## 12. Reference-list rules

Every in-text citation must have a matching reference entry, and every reference entry must be cited in the text.

The final thesis reference list must:
- be comprehensive;
- appear before the appendices;
- be alphabetized by first-author surname under APA 7;
- preserve accurate author names, year, title, source, volume/issue, pages/article number, and DOI/URL;
- use DOI URLs in the form `https://doi.org/...` where available;
- use sentence case for article/chapter titles;
- use italics for journal/book titles and journal volume where APA requires;
- apply a hanging indent in the final document.

ASU Graduate College additionally requires individual reference entries to be single-spaced with one additional line space between entries. Chapter-level reference lists are permitted, but a comprehensive reference list is still required before the appendices.

## 14. Reference verification rule

Before final use:
1. resolve the DOI when one exists;
2. verify title, authors, year, venue, volume/issue, pages/article number against the publisher or another authoritative bibliographic source;
3. verify publication status for recent or online-first work;
4. reject fabricated, incomplete, mismatched, or unverifiable references;
5. do not cite a search-result snippet as the scientific source when the underlying paper is available.

Generated citation-manager output is a draft, not evidence that metadata are correct.

## 15. Chapter-I citation architecture

For Chapter 1:

- **1.1 Background:** cite service-centered SoS, P–T coupling mechanisms, and established interdependency foundations.
- **1.2 System context:** cite external ontology/theory and mechanisms; do not cite thesis shorthand merely because it has symbols.
- **1.3 Problem statement:** cite claims about existing methods and known limitations; keep the end-to-end problem statement as thesis synthesis.
- **1.4 Research gap:** use the Established → Limitation → Handoff → Test structure with adjacent evidence.
- **1.5 Sustainable Engineering significance:** cite empirical/theoretical trade-off evidence; keep accounting-boundary choices explicitly thesis-defined.
- **1.6 Scope:** normally dominated by thesis decisions; cite only externally imposed requirements or externally derived factual limits.
- **1.7 Framework:** the seven-stage framework is a thesis synthesis; cite foundations only where a stage relies on an external theory.
- **1.8–1.11 GO/RQ/SO/H:** do not pad the research contract with citations. Evidence justification belongs in surrounding prose, Chapter 2, or the claim/evidence register.
- **1.12 Contributions:** cite established foundations only to delimit novelty; proposed contributions themselves are not externally established claims.
- **1.13 Organization:** no citations normally required.

## 16. Citation QA gate

Before a chapter is frozen:

```math
\boxed{
G_{cite}
=
C_{adj}
\land
C_{match}
\land
C_{meta}
\land
C_{support}
\land
C_{domain}
\land
C_{complete}
}
```

where:

- `C_adj`: citations are adjacent to supported claims;
- `C_match`: every in-text citation matches a reference and vice versa;
- `C_meta`: reference metadata are verified;
- `C_support`: source supports the exact proposition;
- `C_domain`: validity domain/population/system is compatible or transfer is explicitly qualified;
- `C_complete`: all externally sourced substantive claims are cited.

If any component is unresolved, citation status is `BLOCKED`, not `PASS`.

## 17. Governing principle

```math
\boxed{
\operatorname{Strength}(\text{claim})
\le
\operatorname{Strength}(\text{evidence})
}
```

Citation density is not a substitute for scientific support. The objective is **traceable evidence**, not maximum citation count.
