# Thesis Writing Grading Standard V1

**Status:** Active thesis-wide grading authority  
**Effective date:** 2026-09-20  
**Companion workbook:** `thesis/00_governance/SIMPLE_THESIS_WRITING_GRADING.xlsx`

## Purpose

This standard defines the baseline grading method for thesis writing and scientific communication.

Whenever a task asks to **grade, score, rate, assess, evaluate, or compare** thesis writing or thesis-facing scientific communication, this standard must be loaded and applied before assigning an overall grade.

It applies to, at minimum:

- thesis chapters and sections;
- abstracts;
- research-problem and research-gap writing;
- research questions, objectives, and hypothesis wording;
- mathematical/scientific exposition;
- literature-synthesis prose;
- figure or table explanatory text;
- results, discussion, conclusions, and contribution claims;
- alternative draft comparisons.

For technical work such as mathematical models, simulations, code, experiments, data products, or figures, this writing rubric remains a **baseline communication and scientific-argument rubric**. Task-specific technical criteria must be added where needed; they do not replace this baseline.

## Weighted grading rubric

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Useful information | 10% |
| Scientific precision | 15% |
| Academic / human writing | 10% |
| Infrastructure terminology | 10% |
| Thesis alignment | 15% |
| Scope and validity control | 10% |
| Systems thinking | 10% |
| Engineering / design thinking | 5% |
| Research-method rigor | 10% |
| Evidence / argument quality | 5% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

Each criterion is scored from **1 to 10**. The weighted final grade is

[
G=sum_{k=1}^{10} w_krac{s_k}{10},
]

where (w_k) is the percentage weight and (s_kin[1,10]) is the criterion score.

## Grade interpretation

| Final grade | Interpretation | Default decision |
|---|---|---|
| 95–100 | Exceptional thesis writing | Freeze candidate after evidence/governance check |
| 90–94 | Strong thesis writing | Minor revision |
| 85–89 | Good but visibly improvable | Targeted revision |
| 75–84 | Adequate but not ready | Revise |
| <75 | Material writing/scientific weaknesses | Major revision |

## Output requirement for future grading

A thesis grading response should normally report:

1. the overall grade out of 100;
2. the 10 criterion scores;
3. the strongest scientific qualities;
4. the main deductions;
5. the highest-priority revisions;
6. any canonical, scope, evidence, or validity violation;
7. a clear distinction between writing-quality weaknesses and scientific-content weaknesses.

If multiple versions are compared, use the same rubric and weights for every version.

## Governance

This grading standard does not override:

- the canonical Chapter-I authority;
- the canonical RQ/SO/H authority;
- the Scientific Writing and Inference Standard;
- ASU/SSEBE requirements;
- committee or supervisor decisions.

Those authorities define what the thesis is scientifically allowed to claim. This standard evaluates how rigorously that authorized content is written and communicated.
