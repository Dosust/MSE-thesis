# Slide 2 — Research Background

**Main Message:** Power and Electrified Transportation are distinct physical systems connected through electrical charging interfaces, with operating conditions influenced by extreme heat.

- **Power → Transportation:** Distribution networks and transformers supply electricity to EV charging infrastructure.
- **Transportation → Power:** EV charging demand affects distribution-network loading and can influence transformer thermal ageing (Hilshey et al., 2013; Qian et al., 2015).
- **Extreme heat:** Ambient temperature influences equipment thermal conditions, including EVSE operation under extreme climatic conditions (Krama et al., 2026).

**Infographic focus:** Physical transformer–AC interface–EVSE–vehicle configuration, with separate extreme-heat exposure to both systems.

---

# Slide 3 — Research Problem and Gap

**Main Message:** It remains necessary to establish whether heat-conditioned operating changes produce attributable directional interdependencies and service consequences over physical state and time.

- **P → Tₑ:** Transformer thermal conditions may activate applicable operating restrictions that constrain electrical supply and EV charging capability.
- **Tₑ → P:** EVSE thermal-control responses may change grid-side AC demand, transformer loading and subsequent thermal behavior.
- **Scientific gap:** Existing studies address relevant component behavior (Hilshey et al., 2013; Qian et al., 2015; Krama et al., 2026), but the proposed directional coupling and service consequences require independent qualification.

**Research Problem**

How do qualified heat-conditioned operating mechanisms affect interactions between Power and Electrified Transportation systems, and what are their consequences through physical state and time?

**Scientific qualification:** Shared extreme-heat exposure does not establish directional interdependency.

**Infographic focus:** Two distinct conditional pathways, P → Tₑ and Tₑ → P, under common external heat forcing.

# Slide 4 — Research Objectives

**Main Message:** Identify and qualify thermal interdependencies, model their state–time evolution and quantify supported electricity and mobility service consequences.

**General Objective**

To characterize and model thermal interdependencies between the Power System and the Electrified Transportation System under extreme heat, and to quantify how their consequences evolve through physical state and time.

**Specific Objectives**

- **SO1 — Identify and qualify:** Identify and qualify the thermally sensitive physical states and directional thermal interdependency mechanisms connecting the Power System and the Electrified Transportation System.
- **SO2 — Model:** Develop a physics-based state-time model of the qualified thermal interdependencies and their propagation under extreme heat.
- **SO3 — Quantify:** Quantify critical transition times, interdependency propagation delays, and the resulting electricity and mobility service consequences.

**Infographic focus:** Scientific investigation of equipment states and mechanisms, state–time dynamics, and measurable service consequences.

---

# Slide 5 — Research Methodology

**Main Message:** The research combines electrical-network verification, physics-based thermal modeling and independent directional investigations to assess service consequences.

- **Electrical reference models:** Reproduce the unmodified IEEE 13-node network (B0) and develop a documented EVSE-augmented configuration (B1).
- **Physics-based investigation:** Qualify thermal states, operating constraints and grid-side AC interfaces; examine P → Tₑ and Tₑ → P independently under matched conditions.
- **System-level evaluation:** Apply the qualified approach to the IEEE 123-node network to investigate critical transition times, propagation delays, uncertainty and electricity/mobility service consequences.

**Research Sequence**

Evidence → B0/B1 → Thermal Model → Directional Tests → IEEE 123-node Evaluation

**Scientific qualification:** These are proposed methodological stages. Electrical verification, thermal-model validation and causal attribution must be assessed separately.

**Infographic focus:** Physical feeder–transformer–EVSE model, thermal processes and progression from IEEE 13-node development to IEEE 123-node evaluation.

# Slide 6 — Expected Contribution and Engineering Significance

**Main Message:** The research aims to determine when thermal interdependencies affect electricity and mobility services, supporting evidence-based resilience and sustainability assessment.

- **Physical contribution:** Identify evidence-supported directional thermal mechanisms and the conditions under which they produce cross-system effects.
- **Mathematical contribution:** Characterize state–time evolution, critical transition times, propagation delays and service consequences, including cases where no directional effect is established.
- **Engineering contribution:** Inform infrastructure resilience and bounded environmental, economic and social sustainability assessment, with potential relevance to SDGs 7, 9, 11 and 13.

**Expected Research Contribution**

Qualified Mechanisms → State–Time Behavior → Electricity and Mobility Service Consequences → Resilience and Sustainability Assessment

**Sustainable Development Goal Alignment**

- **SDG 7 — Affordable and Clean Energy:** Reliable and sustainable electricity services.
- **SDG 9 — Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure:** Resilient infrastructure and engineering innovation.
- **SDG 11 — Sustainable Cities and Communities:** Reliable electrified mobility and urban services.
- **SDG 13 — Climate Action:** Climate-related infrastructure adaptation and resilience.

**Scientific qualification:** SDG alignment indicates potential relevance, not demonstrated achievement of official SDG targets or indicators.

**Infographic focus:** Realistic coupled infrastructure, possible state–time and service consequences, resilience assessment, sustainability pathways, and the four SDG icons.
