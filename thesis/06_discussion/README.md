# Chapter 6 — Discussion and Sustainable Engineering Implications

**Writing/inference authority:** [`../00_governance/SCIENTIFIC_WRITING_AND_INFERENCE_STANDARD_V1.md`](../00_governance/SCIENTIFIC_WRITING_AND_INFERENCE_STANDARD_V1.md)

## Purpose

Interpret validated results against theory, rival explanations, infrastructure practice and sustainable-engineering objectives without exceeding the tested validity domain.

## Required questions

```math
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{What mechanism is supported by the result?}\\
&\text{What rival explanation remains plausible?}\\
&\text{Under what conditions does the result hold?}\\
&\text{Which assumptions materially affect it?}\\
&\text{With what uncertainty?}\\
&\text{How does it compare with prior evidence?}\\
&\text{What engineering decision changes?}\\
&\text{What remains unvalidated?}
\end{aligned}}
```

## Recommended sections

1. summary of H1–H5 outcomes;
2. mechanistic interpretation;
3. comparison with prior literature;
4. implications for infrastructure-system representation;
5. implications for information, organization and control;
6. implications for viability, reachability and conventional resilience assessment;
7. intervention effectiveness and resource efficiency;
8. sustainability synergies, trade-offs and burden shifting;
9. joint engineering decision implications;
10. transferability and generalization limits;
11. limitations and assumption sensitivity;
12. remaining uncertainty and unresolved questions.

## Discussion discipline

### Result versus interpretation

Chapter V reports the result. Chapter VI explains what the result means and what it does **not** establish.

### Literature comparison

Every comparison with prior research requires an adjacent qualified citation. Agreement with literature does not by itself validate the thesis model; disagreement does not by itself invalidate it.

### Assumption-sensitivity requirement

For every material limitation state:

```math
\boxed{
\text{assumption/data/model limitation}
\rightarrow
\text{affected quantity}
\rightarrow
\text{possible inference consequence}
\rightarrow
\text{sensitivity/mitigation}
\rightarrow
\text{remaining validity domain}.
}
```

Avoid generic statements such as “the study has uncertainty.” Identify the uncertainty source and what conclusion it can change.

### Sustainable-engineering discipline

Sustainability implications must be connected to measured or modeled system behavior and declared accounting boundaries rather than generic policy claims.

Preserve:

```math
\boxed{
\text{resilience}
\neq
\text{effectiveness}
\neq
\text{efficiency}
\neq
\text{sustainability}.
}
```

A resilience-effective intervention may be resource-inefficient or create adverse sustainability consequences; these dimensions must be discussed separately before joint decision interpretation.

### Generalization rule

```math
\boxed{
\text{generalization domain}
\subseteq
\text{what the evidence + validation actually support}.
}
```

Do not generalize a reduced demonstrator, one hazard class, one geography or one organizational model to all coupled infrastructure systems without independent support.

## Exit gate

Chapter VI passes only when every interpretation traces to registered results and qualified evidence, rival explanations are addressed, material assumptions and uncertainty are explicit, sustainability claims are operationally grounded, and generalization does not exceed the validation evidence.
