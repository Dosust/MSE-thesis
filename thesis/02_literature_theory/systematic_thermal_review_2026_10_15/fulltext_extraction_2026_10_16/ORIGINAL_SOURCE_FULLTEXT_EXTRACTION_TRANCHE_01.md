## 1. Godina et al. (2016) — Power thermal ODE + EV demand, ORIGINAL PUBLISHER PDF
**Identity:** DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.enconman.2016.04.087 ; [Eindhoven / TU/e-hosted publisher-version PDF](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/files/51547814/1_s2.0_S019689041630334X_main.pdf) (12 PDF pages; journal pp. 206–216).  
**Physical system:** Distribution transformer loads with additional EV charging scenarios; transformer oil and winding hotspot, external ambient. EV charging is an **electrical demand forcing** on P, not physical heat-conductive transfer at the port.
**Governing source-specific mathematics:** IEC-style thermal transient model. Journal pp. 209–210, §2.3.1 Eq. (8) uses hot-spot decomposition `theta_H(t) = theta_a(t) + Delta_theta_o(t) + Delta_theta_h(t)` for ambient, oil rise, and winding hotspot rise. §2.3.2 Eqs. (9)–(12) apply oil/winding first-order differential equations with load factor `K`, rated heat rise, thermal time constants `tau_o,tau_w`, loss ratio and winding/oil exponents; §2.3.3 integrates thermal aging. Journal p. 209 Eqs. (4)–(7) are exponential response solutions under changed loading; quote journal Eq. (8), not an unverified reinterpretation of inconsistent OCR from Eq. (13).
**Assumptions:** EV demands generated from charging/travel/SOC distributions; addition to modeled distribution transformer loading; standards-style lumped thermal response and ambient inputs.
**Measurement/evidence:** Original grid base-load input measurements used, EV profiles and transformer HST/aging consequences modeled under scenarios, not original measured per-EVSE rectifier/charger heat or independently measured joint P hot-spot field response in this paper.  
**Validation level:** `EVIDENCE_BOUNDED_INPUTS + SIMULATED_THERMAL_OUTPUT`, **not** P↔Tₑ observed directional field validation.
**SO1–SO3 implication:** Tₑ→P *electrical loading→modeled transformer heat* is existing prior art. Coupled extreme-heat EVSE derating, bidirectional thermal actions and actual vehicle mission obligation not established by this source.

## 2. Blomgren et al. (2023) — Observed transformer lid, stochastic RC thermal ODEs, ORIGINAL PUBLISHER PDF
**Identity:** DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.segan.2023.101048 ; [DTU Orbit publisher PDF](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/320475870/1_s2.0_S2352467723000565_main.pdf) (15 pages including cover).
**Physical system:** Real Danish outdoor 10/0.4 kV oil-cooled distribution transformers: 400 kVA and 200 kVA, each serving residential customers (some owning EVs). **No matched EVSE temperature/control logs**, so do not infer coupled field observation.
**Governing mathematics:** Continuous–discrete stochastic state-space, journal p.5 §4.1 Eqs. (1)–(2): `dx_t=(Ax_t+Bu_t)dt + Sigma dW_t; y_k=C x_k+e_k`. Conservation-based §4.2.1 Eq. (3), `C dT/dt = Phi_gain - Phi_loss`. Journal p.7 §4.3.1 Eq. (7) one-lid-state heat balance `dT_i=[Phi_h+(T_a-T_i)/R_ia]dt/C_i + sigma dw`, `Phi_h=a I_3ph^2 + b`; Eq. (8) `T_lid,k = T_i,k + e_k`. §4.3.2 Eqs. (9)–(11) extend to latent internal temperature and lid coupling via `R_ti,C_t`. This **internal latent state is explicitly NOT identified as the winding hotspot**.
**Assumptions:** Empirical RC resistance/capacitance; internal losses parameterized by three-phase current squared plus no-load term; stochastic Wiener state noise and observation error; ambient and, for extended models, weather/wind/solar; parameters estimated using measured load/lid trajectories.
**Measured target and resolution:** Field transformer *lid* and radiator/housing temperatures, measured three-phase electrical inputs; electrical acquisition 1-s aggregated for modeling; meteorological inputs at 10-min resolution aggregated to 30 min; P sensors on 2 separate LV feeders. **The paper validates `T_lid`, NOT internal winding hotspot.**
**Validation:** Model selection/residual checks and held-out temperature forecasting vs actual measured lid. Reported 6h prediction mean absolute error ~0.4–0.6 °C. Observational fit is **P component validation** only, not actual thermal interface action or heat-to-transport service effect.
**SO1–SO3:** Strong defensible P component state-time baseline, measurement semantic discipline, stochastic UQ; cannot substitute measured lid for direct HST or assume P→Tₑ curtailment.

## 3. Bragone et al. (2022) — 1D heat PDE + PINN against FVM, ORIGINAL JOURNAL PDF
**Identity:** DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsr.2022.108447 ; [DiVA publisher PDF](https://www.diva-portal.org/smash/get/diva2:1683236/FULLTEXT01.pdf) (7 PDF pages).
**Governing mathematics (journal p.2 Eq. 1):** `rho c_p partial_t T = k partial_xx T + q`, 1D transient heat diffusion; Eq. (2) gives an **effective** heating/cooling source `q = P_0 + P_K(t) - h[T(x,t)-T_a(t)]`. Eq. (3) sets vertical-boundary approximations `T(0,t)=T_a(t)`, `T(H,t)=T_o(t)`, with normalized `H=1` and measured ambient/top-oil/load factor boundary inputs. PINN minimizes observation/boundary errors and PDE residuals at collocation points; finite-volume solution used as reference.
**Mathematical implementation warning:** Published `P_0,P_K` appear as watts, `h` as W/m² K, `k` as W/m K whereas the local 1D PDE source `q` needs consistent volumetric/geometry scaling. The article treats these as effective simplifications; **do not transplant the equation or numerical coefficients without specifying effective geometry and unit normalization**. Authors note effective conductivity and convection terms do not resolve buoyant oil motion or actual full transformer geometry (journal p.3).
**Measured vs numerical:** Real transformer ambient temperature, top-oil and load factor feed the problem; unobserved internal vertical temperature solution is **PINN compared against FVM**, not against a measured full internal spatial temperature field. Results on first 100 h; best relative L2 field error about 0.145 vs FVM, and top-oil data relative error about 0.0259 (journal p.4). 
**Evidence label:** `MEASURED_BOUNDARY_INPUTS + NUMERICAL_REFERENCE_COMPARISON`; **NOT** independent physical-field validation of a full spatial state.
**SO1–SO3:** PINNs and transformer thermal PDEs are prior art; learn heat-equation residual design and limits but do not select PINN before appropriate measured targets and baseline superiority are shown.

## 4. Balogun et al., EV-EcoSim (2024) — Multi-timescale P–Tₑ co-simulation, ORIGINAL AUTHOR MANUSCRIPT
**Identity:** journal DOI https://doi.org/10.1109/TSG.2023.3339374 ; **version examined** [arXiv:2401.04705v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.04705) (13 pages). **The journal publisher version was NOT independently reconciled** to this author manuscript.
**Governing mathematics:** Manuscript §II.D p.6 Eqs. (13)–(14) approximate transformer top-oil and hotspot temperature ODEs with ambient heat input, load factor `K(t)`, loss ratio, rated rises and thermal time constants; forward Euler integration. §II.F Eq. (16) at p.6 provides AC/EVSE charging port active-power relationship `P_grid = P_ev / eta_evse`, while Eq. (17) gives reactive-power model from power factor. Simulation exchanges these EVSE node injections with quasi-static distribution power-flow using GridLAB-D (§II.G).
**Important source assumption:** Battery ECM parameter fitting uses battery test data, but studied cells are assumed at a **controlled ambient 23 °C** and *internal battery thermal states are not modeled* in this version (manuscript p.3 §II.A, after Eq. 1–4). Consequently this paper cannot be counted as evidence of heat-induced EVSE thermal limiter activation or summer ambient propagation.
**Evidence:** Battery equivalent-circuit model parameters are fit to experimental cell data; grid and transformer/charger integration reported as modeled **co-simulation**, not a matched thermal field experiment at a real charger/transformer installation. Cost/battery-sizing outputs are simulation scenarios, not direct observed mobility mission delivery.
**SO1–SO3:** A coupled power/charging architecture, transformer heat ODEs and EVSE electrical power interface already exist. Potential novelty must involve stronger heat-conditioned physical action, timing, service evidence and/or mathematical justification rather than co-simulation alone.

## 5. Fletcher and Khan / RACE for 2030 (2025) — Heat-limited EV charging, ORIGINAL TECHNICAL REPORT PDF
**Identity:** `RACE Project 23.ET8.R.0571`, March 2025; [official 32-page report](https://www.racefor2030.com.au/content/uploads/Final-report-0571-FINAL-FINAL.pdf), pp. 10–12 and 24–26 especially.
**Governing mechanics:** IEC 60076-7 transformer thermal model in a MATLAB time-step scheme, with transformer oil/hotspot transients, EV charging duty cycles, non-EV utilization and ambient heat. Demonstrated example normalized to an illustrative 1 MVA transformer and combinations of ~250-kW fast chargers. No new physical first-principles law claimed.
**Control law:** Report PDF p.25 `Control strategy`: **four-level hotspot-temperature hysteresis**, reducing available EV charging power between 0% and 100% in 25% increments as modeled hotspot thresholds are reached; delayed transformer thermal state conditions constrain EV charging deliveries. Specific threshold values are *case assumptions*, not generally approved setpoints.
**Evidence:** Part 1 is desktop/simulation feasibility **informed by heat-run tests and standards**. Project Part 2 discusses future instrumented mineral-oil and ester-oil transformer trials, but completion/public data from this planned phase is **not established by this source**.
**Validation:** Source supplies physical-standard-/test-informed model **plus simulations**, not independently observed same-site transformer-triggered charging curtailment and actual EV mission fulfillment. Shows mathematically relevant modeled P→Tₑ power limitation and charge energy service proxy.
**Research-gap consequence:** **CRITICAL COUNTEREXAMPLE:** A time-dependent transformer heat state **already** conditions EV charging capability and delivered energy in prior research. Any novelty proposal saying nobody modeled P-temperature→EV charging service is false. Test actual field activation/receiver response and separately qualified Tₑ→P thermal mechanism instead.

## 6. Villarroel, Liu and Wang (2017) — P internal hotspot with physical sensors, PUBLISHER-INDEXED ARTICLE
**Identity:** DOI https://doi.org/10.1049/oap-cired.2017.1102 ; [IET CIRED original article](https://doi.org/10.1049/oap-cired.2017.1102). Access: original publisher-indexed full-text excerpt/section analysis; **an independent original PDF was not acquired**.
**Mechanism and mathematics:** Dynamic IEC thermal loading model with experimentally identified transformer heat parameters from step-loading of a 200-kVA 11/0.433-kV ONAN transformer. 16 **internal fiber-optic sensors**; calibration at 0.7, 1.0 and 1.25 pu, independent 1.8-pu two-hour overload test comparing predicted to directly **measured winding HST**.
**Limitations:** Single lab P transformer, no connected EVSE, no mobility service. Genuine observed P hotspot validation does not establish cross-system coupling. Source exact numeric parameter/coefficient extraction remains incomplete without reproducible original PDF visual page inspection.

## 8. Cross-source physical and mathematical interpretation — not a model freeze

Observed/measured component states and governing *candidate* balances:

- P thermal state `T_P` governed by source `Q_loss,P(I,V,...)` and ambient/other heat rejection; measured lid, measured hotspot and model latent hotspot are distinct.
- EVSE Tₑ component heat state `T_E` requires separately identified semiconductor/cabinet thermal loss/rejection and actual thermal derating flag; public EVSE data frequently observe `P_AC,P_DC` but not `T_E`.
- Conditional P→Tₑ interface: **measured P physical heat state -> verified active temperature-conditioned utility limit -> changed AC EVSE availability -> observed charging capability -> independently defined service**. A modeled 2025 threshold example is established prior art, not field confirmation.
- Conditional Tₑ→P interface: **measured EVSE heat -> activated thermal derating -> observed EVSE AC power/current change -> changed upstream transformer loading/loss -> observed subsequent P temperature**, controlling other demand and ambient; sign of thermal effect may be cooling/delayed rebound.
- State-time `T_P(t),T_E(t)` and threshold time alone is NOT causal propagation proof; verify source action and receiving response, variable units and time sync.
- Mobility requirement: `kWh` delivered or charging minutes alone are proxy outcomes; actual departure/trip commitment must be independently defined.

### Model-selection decision

No PINN/grey-box/PHY family is selected. At this stage **physics determines the state equations**, observations determine calibratability/validation, and any hybrid method must beat verified physics-only baselines on matched independent physical targets and event timing. Absent such data, proceed only with bounds-conscious pure physics model under Professor Pat Phelan's conditional recommendation.
