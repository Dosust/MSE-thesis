# E-CONTEXT-HEAT-001 — Mitiga Solutions (2024)

**Status:** ADMITTED AS CONTEXTUAL / MOTIVATIONAL EVIDENCE ONLY  
**Date admitted:** 2026-10-05  
**Source type:** Industry climate-risk article; not peer-reviewed scientific evidence  
**Primary use:** Slide 1 motivation and proactive-resilience framing

## Source
Mitiga Solutions, "How heatwaves impact infrastructures and ecosystems," June 17, 2024.

## What this source supports

The article frames extreme heat as a growing infrastructure and business risk and emphasizes:
- infrastructure and asset vulnerability to extreme temperatures;
- impacts on cooling systems, materials, workers, and business continuity;
- supply-chain and critical-infrastructure disruption;
- the need to assess which assets are exposed and vulnerable;
- the value of proactive climate-risk planning and adaptation;
- the importance of using climate-risk information to support decisions.

## Thesis relevance

The strongest thesis-compatible message is:

```text
Understand physical climate risk
→ identify vulnerable assets/functions
→ plan improvements
→ adapt operations
→ build resilience
```

This aligns with the thesis-defined resilience cycle:

```text
IMPROVE → ADAPT → RESTORE → LEARN
```

especially the **IMPROVE** stage, because the article explicitly argues for proactive vulnerability assessment and climate-proofing before losses occur.

## Presentation mapping

- **Slide 1 — Problem & motivation:** contextual statement that extreme heat creates infrastructure, operational and economic risk.
- **Slide 8 — Engineering value:** contextual support for proactive, decision-oriented resilience planning.

## What this source does NOT establish

Do not use this article as evidence for:
- power-system thermal equations;
- transport electrification physics;
- P-T interdependency mechanisms;
- quantitative resilience metrics;
- exact heatwave projections;
- model parameters;
- validation;
- novelty claims.

Those require peer-reviewed literature, standards, or authoritative datasets.

## Admission decision

```text
ADMIT:
context, motivation, proactive-risk framing, asset-level decision relevance

HOLD:
quoted cost/benefit figures unless traced to primary sources

REJECT AS CORE SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE:

```
