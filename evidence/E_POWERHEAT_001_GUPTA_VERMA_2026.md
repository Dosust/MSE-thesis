# E-POWERHEAT-001 — Gupta & Verma (2026)

**Status:** ADMITTED EXTERNAL EVIDENCE  
**Use:** Power-side extreme-heat mechanism and presentation development

## Source
A. K. Gupta and K. Verma (2026), "Dynamic thermal impact of heatwaves on resilience of power system considering transmission line and generators capacities," Sustainable Energy, Grids and Networks, 45, 102137.

## Supported mechanism

```text
heatwave / ambient conditions
→ component thermal constraints
→ available transmission and generation capability
→ feasible grid operation
→ load curtailment / electrical service deficit
→ resilience / reliability metrics
```

The paper uses weather-dependent transmission-line ampacity, temperature-dependent generator derating, AC optimal power flow, and service/reliability measures including EENS, LOLE and SOP.

## Thesis use

### ADMIT
- extreme heat can alter electrical-system capability through physical thermal mechanisms;
- line capability may be represented as weather/state dependent rather than fixed;
- generator capability may be temperature dependent;
- physical capability changes can propagate to unserved electrical demand;
- EENS is an example of a service-deficit quantity;
- sensitivity analysis is needed because thermal-model assumptions can alter system conclusions.

### DO NOT TRANSFER DIRECTLY
- paper-specific thresholds;
- generator derating slopes;
- IEEE 57-bus numerical results;
- component bottleneck identities;
- study-specific climate scenarios.

These require independent qualification for the thesis case.

## Presentation mapping

- **Slide 1:** heat can physically constrain electrical service capability.
