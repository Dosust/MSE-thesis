# Data Dictionary and Boundary Rules

## 1. Unit of analysis

The fundamental unit is one focal publication. Do not treat one experiment, one mix, one equation, one framework, or one cited reference as the paper-level unit.

For paper $i$:

$$
\Omega_i=(P_i,F_i,S_i,D_i)
$$

A paper may contain multiple evidence records in each domain.

---

## 2. Original screening columns K–M

### K — Construction / built environment

Binary coding:

$$
K_i=\begin{cases}
1,&\text{direct focal application to construction/built environment}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

Include construction materials, structures, buildings, façades, foundations, bridges, roads/pavements, civil infrastructure, refurbishment, repair, maintenance, demolition, modular/prefabricated construction, or direct built-asset operation.

Do not code Yes merely because construction is mentioned in background literature or because a material could theoretically be used in construction.

### L / SM — Sustainability evaluation method directly implemented

Internally use `SM` to avoid confusion with the linkage variable $\lambda$.

$$
SM_i=\begin{cases}
1,&\text{structured sustainability method/tool is directly implemented by focal authors}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

Potential method families include LCA, LCC, S-LCA, LCSA, MIVES, AHP, TOPSIS, ELECTRE, emergy, structured sustainability indices, integrated assessment, or an author-developed operational sustainability framework.

A method mentioned only in the introduction, literature review, future work, or references does not satisfy SM.

### M — Quantifiable sustainability output

$$
M_i=\begin{cases}
1,&\text{at least one quantified sustainability output is generated}\\
0,&\text{otherwise}
\end{cases}
$$

Eligible outputs include environmental, economic, social, or integrated sustainability quantities/scales generated by the implemented method. Engineering results such as MPa, drift, rutting, thermal conductivity, etc. are not sustainability outputs by themselves.

### K–L–M gate

$$
G_i^{KLM}=K_iSM_iM_i
$$

Only $G_i^{KLM}=1$ proceeds to full N–T extraction in the original protocol.

---

## 3. Columns N–T

### N — Performance effects

Extract focal-author engineering/functional performance effects of the evaluated material, component, structure, building, pavement, foundation, system, etc.

Controlled families include mechanical, structural, durability, service life, thermal, energy, acoustic, fire, geotechnical, pavement, functional/serviceability, reliability/resilience, and optimization/surrogate performance.

Do not place GWP, LCC, social score, etc. in N unless the authors explicitly define them as part of an integrated performance construct; even then retain the original sustainability domain separately.

### O — Core elements of the sustainability evaluation method

Extract components of the evaluation/assessment method, not components of the manuscript or physical construction system. Preserve author terminology.

Examples:
- LCA: functional unit, system boundary, inventory, impact categories/method, interpretation.
- AHP: goal, criteria, subcriteria, alternatives, pairwise matrix, weights, consistency.
- MIVES: requirements, criteria, indicators, value functions, weights, aggregation, index.
- ML assessment: dataset, predictors, target, model, training, validation, prediction/output.

### P — Procedure

Record the workflow of the sustainability evaluation method, not the paper structure or laboratory specimen-preparation sequence unless such a step is part of the assessment tool itself.

Representation:

$$
s_1\rightarrow s_2\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow s_k
$$

### Q — Construction strategy

Identify the actual focal alternative/intervention being evaluated. Keep the summary short, but preserve alternative identity because it is essential for matching engineering and sustainability evidence.

### R — What is evaluated

Separate outcomes into:

$$
R_i=(R_i^P,R_i^{Env},R_i^{Eco},R_i^{Soc},R_i^{Int})
$$

where $P$ is engineering/functional performance and the remaining fields are environmental, economic, social, and integrated sustainability outcomes.

### S — Validation

Validation concerns the sustainability assessment method/tool/framework, not merely whether the paper contains experiments.

Use:

$$
V_i\in\{No,Applied,InternallyValidated,ExternallyValidated\}
$$

A case study is not automatically validation; it may be demonstration/application only.

### T — Backward snowballing

Extract only identifiable sustainability evaluation methods, tools, frameworks, indices, rating systems, or decision methods useful for backward snowballing. Do not use T as a general keyword list.

---

## 4. P — Engineering-performance evidence object

For each engineering evidence record:

$$
P_{ip}=(Family,Metric,Method,Result,Unit,Alternative,Comparator,Origin)
$$

Strong evidence should be focal-author generated by experiment, simulation, monitoring, or analytical calculation.

Secondary, supplier-provided, assumed, or literature-derived values do not satisfy strong author-generated engineering evidence.

---

## 5. F — Integration object

For each integration record:

$$
F_{if}=(Input,Transformation,Output,AlternativeMapping,FormulaType)
$$

The central question is what operation transforms, constrains, normalizes, weights, aggregates, or otherwise connects engineering performance with sustainability.

### Traceable-alternative correspondence

Use traceable correspondence, not exact set equality:

$$
TA_i=\mathbb{1}[\text{engineering and sustainability evidence can be matched at alternative level}]
$$

Exact equality of engineering and sustainability alternative sets is not required. For example, engineering screening may eliminate an alternative before the final sustainability comparison while retaining traceable correspondence.

### Integration levels

- $\lambda=0$: no usable linkage, missing domain, unrelated alternatives, or $TA=0$.
- $\lambda=1$: matched parallel evidence, $P\parallel S$.
- $\lambda=2$: directional analytical coupling, $P\rightarrow S$; performance changes/defines/constrains sustainability assessment.
- $\lambda=3$: explicit author-implemented integrated decision architecture.

Maximum integration may be supported by an author algebraic formula or an explicit author-implemented causal/process architecture. A reviewer-created formula never establishes $\lambda=3$.

Formula-type codes:
- `AF` = author algebraic formula.
- `AC` = author causal/process architecture.
- `RR` = reviewer reconstruction.
- `NF` = no explicit qualifying formula/architecture.

---

## 6. S — Sustainability evidence object

For every quantified sustainability record:

$$
S_{is}=(Method,Dimension,Indicator,Result,Unit,FunctionalBasis,Alternative,Origin)
$$

Dimensions:

$$
S_i=(S_i^{Env},S_i^{Eco},S_i^{Soc},S_i^{Int})
$$

Method name alone is not a result. Store the actual output generated by the implemented method.

---

## 7. D — Decision object

A decision record is:

$$
D_{id}=(Type,Alternatives,Rule,Outcome,Sensitivity/Robustness)
$$

Decision types include ranking, selection, rejection, optimization, prioritization, Pareto/trade-off, robust solution, scenario preference, and design recommendation.

A comparative result alone (e.g. “B has lower emissions”) is not automatically a decision.

---

## 8. R1–R4 scoring boundaries

### R1 — depth/completeness of implemented sustainability method

Broad scale:
- 0 = none.
- 1 = informal/weak sustainability calculation.
- 2 = structured method implemented but incompletely operationalized.
- 3 = structured method clearly operationalized and directly applied.

For KLM-eligible papers, R1 normally distinguishes 2 versus 3 rather than re-asking whether a method exists.

### R2 — methodological reproducibility

R2 is method-family specific and must not be mechanically assigned from a generic percentage threshold.

- 0 = not reproducible.
- 1 = fragmentary configuration.
- 2 = major configuration identifiable but essential gaps remain.
- 3 = essential method configuration sufficiently reproducible.

An auxiliary reporting-coverage statistic may be computed, but it supports rather than determines R2.

### R3 — author-generated engineering evidence

- 0 = none.
- 1 = secondary/assumed/qualitative.
- 2 = author-generated quantitative evidence with incomplete reporting.
- 3 = author-generated and methodologically strong engineering evidence.

### R4 — sustainability-output strength

- 0 = none.
- 1 = qualitative/weak claim.
- 2 = quantified output exists but coverage/specification is limited.
- 3 = clear quantitative output generated by the implemented sustainability method with traceable indicator/alternative/basis.

---

## 9. Evidence states and provenance

Every substantive field should retain a value plus evidence state:

- `SUPPORTED` — explicit source support.
- `NR` — investigated but not reported.
- `U` — genuinely unclear/ambiguous.
- `NA` — not applicable.

Provenance classes should distinguish focal-author generated, focal-author implemented, focal-author validated, established method applied, secondary/cited, supplier, assumed, discussed, NR, U, and NA.

Blank is not equivalent to zero. `NR`, `U`, `NA`, and coded absence must remain distinct.

---

## 10. Evidence-first rule

The authoritative sequence is:

$$
\boxed{
Keyword\rightarrow Candidate\ Passage\rightarrow Author\ Action\rightarrow Equation/Procedure\rightarrow Evidence\ Code\rightarrow Interpretation\rightarrow Score
}
$$

No score is allowed to create its own supporting evidence retroactively.
