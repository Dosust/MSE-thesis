## 1. Reason for addendum

A targeted second-stage search asked a harder question than the original F1/F2 pass:

```math
\boxed{
\text{Has viability theory already been applied to infrastructure, power, transportation, resilience and intervention?}

```

The answer is **yes**. Therefore the thesis must not claim novelty for applying viability theory to engineered infrastructure or resilience in general.

## 2. Closest prior art found

### 2.1 Codependent infrastructure + viability theory

Van Arsdale & Mukherjee (2014), *A Mathematical Framework to Assess Vulnerabilities in Codependent Infrastructure and Natural System Networks*, Construction Research Congress 2014, DOI `10.1061/9780784413517.120`.

Public abstract evidence states that the paper:

```text
represents codependent infrastructure and natural systems as an integrated network system;
develops resilience/robustness metrics from network connection and flow properties;
uses a mathematical framework founded in system dynamics and viability theory.
```

**Consequence:** viability + codependent infrastructure + system dynamics is prior art.

### 2.2 Power-grid safe operating / survivability analysis

Hellmann, Schultz, Grabow, Heitzig & Kurths (2016), *Survivability of deterministic dynamical systems*, Scientific Reports 6, 29654, DOI `10.1038/srep29654`.

The paper explicitly relates desirable-state regions to the viability kernel/safe operating space and applies transient survivability analysis to realistic power-grid models.

**Consequence:** safe-set/viability-related transient resilience analysis in power systems is prior art.

### 2.3 Transportation / vehicle viability kernel

Liu et al. (2018), *Road safety analysis for high-speed vehicle in complex environments based on the viability kernel*, IET Intelligent Transport Systems, DOI `10.1049/iet-its.2017.0168`.

The paper computes/uses a viability kernel and viability regulation map to constrain MPC and guarantee vehicle safety.

**Consequence:** viability-kernel methods in transportation dynamics are prior art, even if not at infrastructure-network scale.

### 2.4 Viability as resilience and intervention/investment logic

Karacaoglu & Krawczyk (2021), *Public policy, systemic resilience and viability theory*, Metroeconomica 72(4):826–848, DOI `10.1111/meca.12349`.

The paper treats viability-kernel width as a resilience measure and discusses investments/policies intended to expand the viability kernel.

**Consequence:** generic “use viability to guide resilience investment” is prior art.

### 2.5 Robust infrastructure/energy viability continues to develop

Recent examples found include Hamilton–Jacobi reachability for viability of constrained waste-to-energy systems under adversarial uncertainty (European Journal of Control, 2026, DOI `10.1016/j.ejcon.2026.101501`) and other energy-system viability formulations.

**Consequence:** robust viability under infrastructure/energy uncertainty is an active field and cannot be presented as an empty research space.

## 3. Additional claims removed from novelty space

The thesis shall not claim novelty for any of the following in isolation:

```text
applying viability theory to infrastructure;
using a viability/safe operating region in power systems;
using viability kernels in transportation control;

robust viability under uncertain energy/infrastructure dynamics.
```
