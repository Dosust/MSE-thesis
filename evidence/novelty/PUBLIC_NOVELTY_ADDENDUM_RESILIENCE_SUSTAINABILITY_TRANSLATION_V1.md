# Public Novelty Addendum — Resilience-to-Sustainability Translation v1

**Date:** 2026-09-16  
**Status:** PUBLIC/SCHOLARLY FALSIFICATION ADDENDUM — GLOBAL NOVELTY NOT CERTIFIED  
**Purpose:** subtract prior art from the proposed resilience-to-sustainability bridge before any thesis contribution claim is made.

## 1. Broad claim tested

> Linking infrastructure resilience metrics or resilience assessment to sustainability outcomes and decisions is novel.

### Decision

```math
\boxed{\text{FALSIFIED}.}
```

The literature already contains:
- conceptual frameworks in which resilience contributes to sustainability, sustainability contributes to resilience, or both are treated as distinct but interacting objectives;
- quantitative infrastructure studies linking functionality loss, downtime, repair, traffic detours, travel time/distance, repair cost and environmental impacts;
- integrated resilience–sustainability decision frameworks using lifecycle assessment, lifecycle cost, multiobjective optimization and multicriteria methods.

Therefore the thesis must not claim novelty for the generic mapping

```math
\text{resilience}\rightarrow\text{sustainability}
```

or for combining resilience and sustainability metrics in one decision framework.

## 2. Representative evidence anchors

1. Di Bari, Cucuzza, Domaneschi & Mitoulis (2025), *Enhancing Sustainability and Resilience Against Natural Hazard of the Built Environment—State of the Art and Development of a Novel Framework*, Sustainable Development, DOI `10.1002/sd.70150`. Their review distinguishes resilience and sustainability as separate concepts, discusses directional and integrated relationships, and identifies quantitative studies connecting functionality/downtime/travel effects to environmental, economic and social consequences.
2. Wang et al. (2018), *A Unified Assessment Approach for Urban Infrastructure Sustainability and Resilience*, DOI `10.1155/2018/2073968`, integrates sustainability and resilience indicators for urban infrastructure.
3. Existing thesis evidence review archetype: *Linking seismic resilience into sustainability assessment of limited-ductility RC buildings*, DOI `10.1016/j.engstruct.2019.03.021`, with the causal architecture

```math
Hazard\rightarrow Structural\ Response\rightarrow Damage\rightarrow Loss\rightarrow\{Repair\ Cost,Downtime,Energy/CO_2\}.
```

These sources are sufficient to reject a broad originality claim for the resilience–sustainability bridge itself.

```math
\boxed{

The thesis contribution, if established, would require all of the following:
1. evidence-qualified P–T–I–O dependency mechanisms;
2. explicit executable dynamics rather than score-only relations;
3. separately defined resilience diagnostics and sustainability consequence functionals;
4. uncertainty-aware parameterization and validation;
5. matched intervention comparisons showing decision value;
6. no arbitrary collapse of resilience and sustainability into one score unless the aggregation rule is independently justified.

```

## 4. Important mathematical boundary

The thesis must not assume

```math
\mathbf J_{\rm sus}=f(\mathbf R)
```

as a universal relationship.

A defensible architecture is

```math
\theta_e\rightarrow Y_{0:T}\rightarrow\{\mathbf R,\mathbf J_{\rm sus}\},
```

with a direct `R -> J_sus` relation used only when a physical or empirical transformation is independently supported.

This boundary is consistent with prior work showing that some resilience metrics influence sustainability consequences while other resilience and sustainability metrics remain separate or trade off against each other.
