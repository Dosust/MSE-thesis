## Dynamic charger mechanism

```math
q_c(k+1)=\max\{0,q_c(k)+\Delta t[\lambda_c(k)-\mu_c(k)]\}.
```

Requested charging power includes both current arrivals and accumulated backlog. Requested EV electricity remains in the power-service denominator even when the charger is de-energized.

For constant energized capacity,

```math
\mu_{\max,c}=\frac{\eta_c\bar p_c}{E_c}.
```

The deterministic queue clears only if `lambda < mu_max`; otherwise backlog grows at `lambda-mu_max`.

## Closed-form verification

Persistent overload benchmark: `lambda=900 veh/h`, `mu_max=720 veh/h`, so predicted queue growth is `180 veh/h`. Over `1.8 h`, predicted terminal backlog is `324 vehicles`; simulation gives `324.0000000000011`.

Temporary-outage benchmark: `0.2 h` outage at `600 veh/h` creates `120 vehicles` of backlog. Post-restoration spare capacity is `720-600=120 veh/h`, so predicted clearance time is exactly `1.0 h`. Power returns at `t=0.6 h`; the simulated M2 queue clears at `t=1.6 h`, while M1 recovers immediately.

## Dynamic road-to-repair mechanism

Crew travel is an explicit state driven by current CTM occupancy and road availability. Under empty available roads, the dynamic path-time mapping reduces exactly to the frozen M1 static free-flow delay. Blocking produces infinite access time. Congestion cannot accelerate travel.

Matched recovery benchmark S3R:

- static M1-style free-flow restoration: `0.14 h`;
- dynamic M2 restoration with initially congested roads: `0.19 h`.

The power network and repair productivity are unchanged; the difference is generated by state-dependent access delay.
