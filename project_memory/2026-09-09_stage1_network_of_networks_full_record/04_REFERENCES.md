# Core References

1. **Rinaldi, S. M., Peerenboom, J. P., & Kelly, T. K. (2001).** Identifying, understanding, and analyzing critical infrastructure interdependencies. *IEEE Control Systems Magazine*, 21(6), 11-25. DOI: 10.1109/37.969131. Use for dependency/interdependency definitions and physical/cyber/geographic/logical taxonomy.

2. **Ouyang, M. (2014).** Review on modeling and simulation of interdependent critical infrastructure systems. *Reliability Engineering & System Safety*, 121, 43-60. DOI: 10.1016/j.ress.2013.06.040. Use for interdependent-infrastructure modeling classes and terminology discipline.

3. **Kivela, M., Arenas, A., Barthelemy, M., Gleeson, J. P., Moreno, Y., & Porter, M. A. (2014).** Multilayer networks. *Journal of Complex Networks*, 2(3), 203-271. DOI: 10.1093/comnet/cnu016. Use for heterogeneous multilayer/network-of-networks representation and supra-adjacency concepts.

4. **De Domenico, M., Sole-Ribalta, A., Cozzo, E., Kivela, M., Moreno, Y., Porter, M. A., Gomez, S., & Arenas, A. (2013).** Mathematical formulation of multilayer networks. *Physical Review X*, 3, 041022. DOI: 10.1103/PhysRevX.3.041022. Use for tensor/multidimensional representation of multilayer connectivity.

5. **Gopstein, A., Nguyen, C., O'Fallon, C., Hastings, N., & Wollman, D. (2021).** NIST Framework and Roadmap for Smart Grid Interoperability Standards, Release 4.0. NIST SP 1108r4. DOI: 10.6028/NIST.SP.1108r4. Use for smart-grid domain/component boundaries, DER, distribution, operations, communication and interoperability.

6. **Griffor, E. R., Greer, C., Wollman, D., & Burns, M. (2017).** Framework for Cyber-Physical Systems: Volume 1, Overview. NIST SP 1500-201. DOI: 10.6028/NIST.SP.1500-201. Use for explicit physical-computation-communication-sensing-actuation-human decomposition.

7. **Fotouhi, H., Moryadee, S., & Miller-Hooks, E. (2017).** Quantifying the resilience of an urban traffic-electric power coupled system. *Reliability Engineering & System Safety*, 163, 79-94. DOI: 10.1016/j.ress.2017.01.026. Use for explicit traffic-power coupling, linking variables and restoration accessibility.

8. **Li, Z., Tang, W., Lian, X., Chen, X., Zhang, W., & Qian, T. (2022).** A resilience-oriented two-stage recovery method for power distribution system considering transportation network. *International Journal of Electrical Power & Energy Systems*, 135, 107497. DOI: 10.1016/j.ijepes.2021.107497. Use for repair-crew/mobile-storage routing and road-constrained distribution-system restoration.

9. **Fan, G., Du, Z., Lin, X., & Chen, N. (2024).** Mobile power sources pre-allocation and dispatch strategy in power-transportation coupled network under extreme weather. *IET Renewable Power Generation*, 18. DOI: 10.1049/rpg2.12872. Use for dynamic P-T information, mobile-power dispatch, road impedance and restoration logistics.

10. **Mohamed, A. A. A. (2019).** On the rising interdependency between the power grid, ICT network, and e-mobility: Modeling and analysis. *Energies*, 12(10), 1874. DOI: 10.3390/en12101874. Use for reciprocal power-ICT-mobility relations and explicit ICT topology.

11. **Rasoulkhani, K., & Mostafavi, A. (2018).** Resilience as an emergent property of human-infrastructure dynamics. *PLOS ONE*, 13(11), e0207674. DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0207674. Use for human-infrastructure coupling and resilience as an emergent system property.

12. **Dong, S., Li, Q., Farahmand, H., Mostafavi, A., Berke, P. R., & Vedlitz, A. (2020).** Institutional Connectedness in Resilience Planning and Management of Interdependent Infrastructure Systems. *Journal of Management in Engineering*, 36(6). DOI: 10.1061/(ASCE)ME.1943-5479.0000839. Use for coordinated actor networks, plans/policies and institutional connectedness.

13. **Yu, D. J., Shin, H. C., et al. (2025).** Logical interdependencies in infrastructure: What are they, how to identify them, and what do they mean for infrastructure risk analysis? *Risk Analysis*, 45(2), 356-375. DOI: 10.1111/risa.16555. Use for rule-mediated logical/institutional interdependency and graph/hypergraph representation.

14. **MATPOWER documentation (current release).** Use for bus/branch/device-port network-model distinctions, state variables, status variables and fixed ratings/impedances.

15. **Eclipse SUMO documentation (current release).** Use for directed road graph, junction/edge/lane distinctions, microscopic vehicles and aggregate traffic measures.

16. **FHWA Transportation Systems Management and Operations guidance.** Use for transportation operational functions including traffic control, incident management, traveler information and emergency operations.

17. **U.S. EPA eGRID.** Use only in Stages 6-7 for electricity generation mix, emissions and emission rates; not as a Stage-1 resilience network layer.

18. **DOE/Argonne GREET.** Use in Stages 6-7 for life-cycle energy, greenhouse-gas, pollutant and resource implications of transformation pathways; not as an observational Stage-1 resilience dataset.

## Reference governance

- Stage 1 references establish structure, topology, interdependency semantics, system boundaries and evidence discipline.
- Stage 2-5 references establish causal mechanisms, dynamics, control and viability.
- Stage 6-7 references establish sustainability translation, life-cycle implications and transformation pathways.
