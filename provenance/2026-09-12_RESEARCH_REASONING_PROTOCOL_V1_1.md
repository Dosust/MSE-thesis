## Purpose

This record documents the adoption of the thesis-wide reasoning protocol governing how claims, methods, equations, simulations, hypotheses, evidence, figures, and engineering recommendations are constructed and evaluated.

The canonical cycle is

```math
\text{LEARN}
\rightarrow
\text{VERIFY}
\rightarrow
\text{FORMULATE}
\rightarrow
\text{DERIVE}
\rightarrow
\text{TEST}
\rightarrow
\text{VALIDATE}
\rightarrow
\text{SYNTHESIZE}
\rightarrow
\text{ADVANCE}.
```

The governing rule is:

> **No claim without a definition, mechanism, evidence, assumptions, test, and boundary of validity.**

## Key methodological commitments

- distinguish `[E]`, `[D]`, `[H]`, `[P]`, `[A]`, and `[U]`;
- formalize verbal claims into variables, relations, constraints, and objectives;
- require an explicit causal/physical mechanism for engineering arguments;
- test proposed methods against competing baselines;
- separate logical consistency, dimensional consistency, mathematical consistency, numerical verification, sensitivity analysis, benchmark comparison, physical plausibility, and empirical validation;
- synthesize agreement, contradiction, methods, assumptions, limitations, and remaining gaps rather than merely collect references;
- advance novelty only after evidence, competing explanations, uncertainty, and falsification conditions are explicit;
- choose methods from the engineering problem and mathematical structure, not by precedent alone;
- use the minimum mathematics necessary to explain the physics correctly, solve the engineering problem, quantify uncertainty, and support a defensible sustainable decision.
