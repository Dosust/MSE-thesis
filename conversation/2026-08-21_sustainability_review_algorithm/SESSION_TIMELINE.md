# Session Timeline

## Phase 1 — Original 216-paper extraction logic

The review began with a 216-paper eligible dataset derived from broader screening. The original extraction architecture used Columns K–T:

- K: construction/built-environment relevance;
- L: directly implemented sustainability evaluation/assessment method;
- M: quantifiable sustainability output;
- N: engineering performance effects;
- O: core elements of the evaluation method;
- P: procedure/workflow of the evaluation method;
- Q: construction strategy;
- R: what is evaluated;
- S: validation of the evaluation framework/tool/method;
- T: backward snowballing of sustainability evaluation methods/tools.

K–M were eligibility gates. N–T were detailed extraction fields for papers passing K=L=M=Yes.

## Phase 2 — PDF acquisition and 202-paper full-text state

The Zotero export process yielded 202 full PDFs from the 216 eligible-paper set. The PDFs were split into batches of ten for transfer and inspection. This distinction became critical:

- 216 = eligible records;
- 202 = full PDFs actually available in the original extracted corpus.

Later, five additional research PDFs were uploaded. They were assessed separately, giving 207 available full papers in total.

## Phase 3 — Spreadsheet/PDF validation

The initial validation vocabulary was:

- YES OK: fully supported;
- PARTIAL OK: generally correct but incomplete/imprecise;
- NO OK: unsupported, incorrect, contradictory, or materially misattributed;
- FULL PDF NOT AVAILABLE: no definitive full-text judgment.

The conversation progressively tightened this logic so that validation had to be based on exact full-text evidence rather than semantic plausibility.

## Phase 4 — Need for a stricter evidence algorithm

The review question evolved beyond simply asking whether sustainability was assessed. The new requirement became: identify papers that contain both rigorous sustainability assessment and author-generated engineering performance, then determine whether the two domains are actually connected.

This produced five methodological variables:

- R1: structured sustainability-method implementation;
- R2: methodological specification/reproducibility;
- R3: author-generated engineering performance;
- R4: quantified sustainability outcomes;
- L: engineering–sustainability linkage.

## Phase 5 — Original hard Tier A gate

The strict original Core rule was:

`R1=3 AND R2>=2 AND R3=3 AND R4=3 AND L>=2`

This was intentionally conjunctive/non-compensatory.

A high total score could not repair a missing core domain.

## Phase 6 — Extended evidence routes

To broaden the analytical set without weakening the Core definition, three controlled Extended routes were introduced:

- B1: complete evidence but parallel engineering/sustainability reporting;
- B2: engineering evidence is genuinely author-generated but incompletely reported;
- B3: sustainability evidence is quantitative and structured but narrower/incompletely specified.

Only one controlled relaxation is allowed relative to Tier A.

## Phase 7 — Evidence provenance and matrix logic

The bio-inspiration example workbook showed that evidence extraction should precede scoring. The discussion separated:

1. framework evidence;
2. engineering-performance evidence;
3. sustainability-method/outcome evidence;
4. linkage evidence.

The final provenance vocabulary became:

- G: generated by focal authors;
- I: implemented by focal authors;
- A: established external method/database applied;
- V: validated by focal authors;
- M: mentioned/discussed;
- C: cited/secondary;
- NR: not reported;
- U: unclear;
- NA: not applicable.

Evidence provenance is not the same as methodological quality.

## Phase 8 — Candidate-pool reconciliation

Several provisional lists were compared. They shared almost all titles but differed in tier assignments. Rather than prematurely force one tier assignment, the final decision was to retain a 31-paper candidate-validation pool and pass every candidate through the same frozen evidence matrix.

Three papers were provisionally upgraded in one reconciliation because they may contain stronger engineering–sustainability integration than previously credited:

- ferronickel-slag cementitious materials;
- binary-blended concretes assessed with MARS-SC;
- design-based pavement LCA using modulus improvement effects.

Two papers remained explicitly disputed pending direct evidence audit:

- hybrid cold-formed modular steel frame;
- construction-waste-based shading-device refurbishment assessment.

## Phase 9 — Final two-axis model

The final model separates:

- methodological eligibility;
- review relevance.

Methodological eligibility is determined first by R1–R4–L route logic. Only eligible papers are then ranked for review relevance through Z1–Z5 and WRS.

Quality Q is a third, separate ranking dimension used within tiers/relevance classes.

## Phase 10 — Dynamic state model

Each paper moves in order through:

S0 Candidate identified -> S1 PDF/full text confirmed -> S2 evidence extracted -> S3 provenance classified -> S4 R1-R4-L scored -> S5 route assigned -> S6 relevance scored -> S7 quality audited -> S8 second review -> S9 final analytical decision.

No state may be skipped.

## Phase 11 — Sustainability-history video

The conversation ended with development of a two-minute educational video explaining the historical evolution and dimensions of sustainability. The central narrative retained:

natural-resource conservation -> environmental protection -> sustainable development -> environmental/economic/social integration -> SDGs/climate action -> circularity/resilience/equity -> integrated sustainability today.
