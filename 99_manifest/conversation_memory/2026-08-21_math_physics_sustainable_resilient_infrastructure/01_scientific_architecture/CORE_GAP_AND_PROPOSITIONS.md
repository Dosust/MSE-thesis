# Core Research Gap, Scientific Propositions, and Novelty Discipline

## Refined gap

The research gap must not be phrased as a broad absence claim such as:
- “service-state mapping has not been done”;
- “recovery coupling has not been done”;
- “Hamilton–Jacobi viability has never been used in infrastructure.”

The conversation identified recent work that narrows/falsifies those broad statements.

The stronger candidate frontier is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{empirically identified time-varying cross-infrastructure interfaces}\\
&+\text{ quantified epistemic uncertainty}\\
&+\text{ people/service-level consequences}\\
&+\text{ sustainable/equitable viability geometry}\\
&+\text{ formally safe recovery/control}\\
&+\text{ AI-assisted scalable computation}.
\end{aligned}}
\]

## Candidate metric for inverse-model impact on viability

\[
E_{inv}=d_H[\mathcal V(\theta^*),\mathcal V(\widehat\theta)].
\]

This asks how much the estimated safe/viable state geometry is displaced because the true interface parameters/topology are not known exactly.

## Existing-results discipline

Recent work discussed in the conversation indicates:
- people-centric service mappings for interdependent infrastructure exist and can change worst-case conclusions;
- recovery-coupling models with tensor/GNN/GA structure exist;
- HJ viability/reachability has been demonstrated for at least a constrained single-sector Waste-to-Energy setting.

Therefore novelty should be claimed at the **relation/composition level**, not by pretending the ingredients themselves are absent.

## Falsifiable proposition family

P1. Dynamic interfaces materially deform viability geometry relative to static coupling.  
P2. Interface identifiability materially affects the reliability of inferred viability.  
P3. Interface/system timescale ratios govern when static coupling approximations are defensible.  
P4. Sustainability/equity constraints deform the viable region beyond technical constraints alone.  
P5. Recovery/capture geometry contains decision information not captured by viability alone.  
P6. Waste–drainage–flood feedback shifts coupled P–W–T–SW resilience boundaries.  
P7. Reduced/AI surrogate models used for decisions must preserve or conservatively bound viability conclusions.

## Theorem-quality ambition

The program should distinguish and, where possible, establish conditions for:

\[
\boxed{
\text{well-posedness}
\rightarrow
\text{existence}
\rightarrow
\text{uniqueness}
\rightarrow
\text{stability}
\rightarrow
\text{sensitivity}
\rightarrow
\text{identifiability}
\rightarrow
\text{computability}
\rightarrow
\text{validation}
}
\]

“Uniqueness” must always identify which object is meant: trajectory, inverse parameter, optimizer, control policy, or viscosity solution.
