## 1. Governing scientific purpose

```math
\boxed{
\text{Understand infrastructure interdependencies rigorously}
\rightarrow
\text{quantify how they shape resilience}
\rightarrow
\text{use those quantified findings to engineer defensible sustainable transformations}.
}
```

The study is not primarily an exercise in inventing a graph, resilience metric, sustainability index, or optimization algorithm. Mathematical objects are admissible only when they support the causal chain below.

## 3. Inclusion rule

Every proposed equation, model component, dataset, metric, intervention or experiment must map to at least one declared edge of

```math
\mathcal D\to\mathbb I\to F_{\mathcal G}^{m}\to\mathbf R\to\Phi_{RS}\to\Delta\mathbf J_{\rm sus}\to\mathbf a^\star.
```

If no such mapping can be established with physical meaning and an evidence/validation route, the object is excluded from the minimal thesis realization.

## 4. Three mathematical levels must remain distinct

### Level 1 — infrastructure physics

```math
\boxed{
\dot Y=F_{\mathcal G}^{m}(Y,u,\eta;\Theta,\mathbb I),
\qquad
Y^+=J_{q\to q'}(Y)
}
```

Purpose: explain what happens physically/functionally under coupled disturbances and controls.

### Level 2 — resilience diagnosis

```math
\boxed{
\mathbf R=\mathcal R[Y(\cdot)]
}
```

Candidate components include service-loss integrals, cascade timing/severity, sustained recovery time, viability margin and posterior viable mass, but each component must earn its place through decision value.

### Level 3 — sustainability transformation

The thesis shall not assume `sustainability = f(resilience score)`. The working transformation is intervention-mediated:

```math
\boxed{
\Delta\mathbf J_{\rm sus}
=
\Phi_{RS}
(\mathbf R,\mathbf a,\mathfrak M,\omega,T_L)
}
```

where `a` changes the infrastructure regime `M`, `omega` is a declared future/scenario state and `T_L` is the sustainability accounting horizon.

## 5. Fundamental non-equivalence

```math
\boxed{
\Delta\mathbf R>0
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\Delta\mathbf J_{\rm sus}>0.
}
```

Prior infrastructure literature already establishes that resilience and sustainability can trade off through recovery time, emissions, lifecycle cost, redundancy and material/resource use. Therefore the thesis must not claim novelty for the generic existence of a resilience–sustainability bridge. The research question is whether validated P–T–I–O mechanisms can be translated into specific, measurable sustainability consequences with demonstrated decision value.

Representative prior-art anchors:

- Faber et al., *Bridging resilience and sustainability — decision analysis for design and management of infrastructure systems*, DOI `10.1080/23789689.2017.1417348`.
- Mitoulis, Bompa & Argyroudis, *Sustainability and climate resilience metrics and trade-offs in transport infrastructure asset recovery*, DOI `10.1016/j.trd.2023.103800`.
- Zhu & Zhang, *Lifecycle resilience and sustainability trade-offs for underground infrastructure under multi-hazard scenarios*, DOI `10.1016/j.ress.2025.111797`.
