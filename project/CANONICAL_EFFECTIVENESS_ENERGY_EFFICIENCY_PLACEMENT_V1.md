## Canonical placement

> **Effectiveness measures whether an engineering response achieves its intended resilience objective by maintaining or recovering required electricity and mobility services.**

```text
Effectiveness → Resilience / Engineering-Response Assessment
```

Effectiveness may be characterized by justified changes in service deficit, minimum service level, disruption duration, recovery time, or another declared resilience-performance quantity. Exact metrics remain **OPEN** until the service/resilience metrics are frozen.

> **Energy efficiency measures useful electricity or mobility service delivered relative to the energy required to provide that service, using a declared system boundary and functional/service basis.**

```text
Energy Efficiency → Environmental Sustainability
```

Generic forms are:

```math
\eta_E=\frac{\text{useful service output}}{\text{energy input}},
\qquad
I_E=\frac{\text{energy input}}{\text{useful service output}}.
```

The exact numerator, denominator, units, system boundary, and functional/service unit remain **OPEN**.

## Sustainability architecture

The two-pathway sustainability framework remains unchanged.

```text
Pathway 1:
Thermal Interdependency
→ Electricity + Mobility Service Consequence
→ Environmental + Economic + Social Consequence

Pathway 2:
Improve / Adapt / Restore
→ Changed State / Capability / Service
→ Environmental + Economic + Social Consequence
```

Environmental sustainability may include energy use, **energy efficiency**, electrical/conversion losses, GHG emissions, and material/resource burden.

Economic sustainability may include operating cost, maintenance/replacement cost, intervention cost, and service-disruption cost.

Social sustainability may include service access, continuity, affordability, equity, and safety where measurable.

## Permanent distinctions

```text
Effectiveness ≠ Energy Efficiency ≠ Sustainability
Effective Resilience Response ≠ Sustainable Response
Resilience Improvement ≠ Sustainability Improvement
```

An intervention may be highly effective at maintaining or recovering service while consuming more energy, producing more emissions, increasing material burden, or increasing cost. Conversely, an energy-efficient intervention may fail to maintain or recover required service.

## SDG rule

```text
Measured Sustainability Indicator
→ Sustainability Dimension
→ Relevant SDG Target / Indicator
```

Energy efficiency may support SDG interpretation only after it is measured as a declared sustainability indicator. Effectiveness is not mapped directly to an SDG merely because it improves resilience.
