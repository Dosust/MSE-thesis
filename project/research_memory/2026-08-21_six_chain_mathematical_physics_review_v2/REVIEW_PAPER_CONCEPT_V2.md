# REVIEW PAPER CONCEPT — CORRECTED

## Working title
**A Mathematical–Physical Ontology of Sustainable and Resilient Infrastructure Systems: From Governing Laws to Societal Learning**

## Central question
**Where, exactly, do mathematics and physics enter the complete science of sustainable and resilient infrastructure systems?**

## Defensible contribution statement
> Existing literature contains recurrent but fragmented scientific layers. This review systematically identifies those layers, maps their mathematical and physical theories, and synthesizes them into six interacting analytical chains spanning physical-system laws, knowledge/inference, stress/consequence, viability/recovery, engineering lifecycle intervention, and societal evaluation/decision/learning.

## What the paper must NOT claim
- that one previous paper already contains the complete framework;
- that the six chains are a universally accepted taxonomy;
- that a citation beside a generic equation proves equation-level provenance;
- that sustainability and resilience are synonyms;
- that optimization is a lifecycle stage.

## Proposed review questions
RQ1. Which scientific layers recur across sustainable/resilient infrastructure literature?
RQ2. Which mathematics/physics is used at each layer?
RQ3. Which layers are normally coupled in existing models, and which remain fragmented?
RQ4. How are interdependencies represented versus how are interfaces mechanistically modeled?
RQ5. How are observations used to identify unknown coupling, state or model structure?
RQ6. How are physical damage and functionality translated to delivered critical services and societal outcomes?
RQ7. Which resilience studies use viability/reachability/recovery-control mathematics rather than only summary metrics?
RQ8. How do lifecycle engineering actions connect to sustainability, equity, economics, governance and deep-uncertainty decision methods?
RQ9. Which cross-chain links are literature-established, weakly treated, or thesis-original?

## Quantitative review outputs
- coverage matrix: papers × layers;
- co-occurrence matrix of layers;
- network graph of cross-layer integrations;
- percentage of papers that bridge physics→service, observation→identification, failure→recovery, resilience→sustainability, or engineering→societal decision;
- temporal trend of mathematical methods;
- sector/hazard coverage;
- evidence-gap map;
- sensitivity of conclusions to inclusion/exclusion rules.
