# Canonical Six-Chain Ontology

## I. Physical System — What is the system?

\[
\boxed{
\text{Physical Laws}
\rightarrow
\text{Components}
\rightarrow
\text{Topology}
\rightarrow
\text{Interfaces}
\rightarrow
\text{States \& Flows}
\rightarrow
\text{Dynamics}
\rightarrow
\text{Cascades}
}
\]

### Layers
1. Physical laws & constitutive foundations
2. Infrastructure components / asset states
3. System representation & topology
4. Interdependencies & interfaces
5. Coupled states, stocks, flows & capacities
6. Nonlinear / hybrid / stochastic dynamics
7. Disturbance propagation & cascading behavior

### Layers
8. Measurement, sensing & monitoring
9. Observability & data quality
10. System identification / inverse problems
11. Calibration / model updating
12. State estimation
13. Data assimilation
14. Parameter/structural/state uncertainty quantification
15. Forecasting / predictive simulation / scenario projection

## III. Stress–Failure–Consequence — What happens under stress?

\[
\boxed{
\text{Hazard}
\rightarrow
\text{Exposure}
\rightarrow
\text{Vulnerability}
\rightarrow
\text{Fragility}
\rightarrow
\text{Damage/Failure}
\rightarrow
\text{Cascade}
\rightarrow
\text{Functional Loss}
\rightarrow
\text{Service Loss}
\rightarrow
\text{Consequences}
\rightarrow
\text{Risk/Criticality}
}
\]

### Critical state-to-society mapping
\[
\boxed{X(t)\rightarrow F(t)\rightarrow S(t)\rightarrow C(t)}
\]

where \(X\)=physical state, \(F\)=functional capability, \(S\)=service delivered, and \(C\)=societal consequence.

## V. Engineering Lifecycle — What can engineers do?

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\text{Requirements}
&\rightarrow\text{Design}
\rightarrow\text{Deploy}
\rightarrow\text{Commission}
\rightarrow\text{Operate}
\rightarrow\text{Monitor}\\
&\rightarrow\text{Age}
\rightarrow\text{Maintain}
\rightarrow\text{Repair/Replace}
\rightarrow\text{Retrofit/Expand}\\
&\rightarrow\text{Plan}
\rightarrow\text{Adapt}
\rightarrow\text{Transform}
\rightarrow\text{Decommission/Circularize}
\end{aligned}}
\]

Optimization is **cross-cutting** across design, operation, control, recovery, maintenance, planning and adaptation.

## Cross-chain feedback

- I ↔ II: physical state ↔ evidence
- II ↔ III: forecast ↔ stress characterization
- III ↔ IV: loss ↔ viability/recovery
- IV ↔ V: control ↔ engineering operation
- V ↔ VI: engineering alternatives ↔ societal values/decisions
- VI → I: implementation/adaptation changes system structure
- VI → II: learning updates priors, models and inference
