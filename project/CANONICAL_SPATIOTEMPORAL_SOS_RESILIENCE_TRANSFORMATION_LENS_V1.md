## 2. Spatiotemporal interpretation

The physical SoS is spatially embedded and evolves through time.

Preferred executable representation remains component/interface indexed:

~~~math
\boxed{
x_i\in\mathbb R^2,\qquad Y_i(t),\qquad \eta(x_i,t).
}
~~~

The shorthand

~~~math
\mathcal G(\mathbf r,t),\qquad
Y(\mathbf r,t),\qquad
\eta(\mathbf r,t)
~~~

is permitted only as interpretive notation. It does not by itself require a continuous field, PDE, or continuously changing topology.

A time-varying graph is used only where topology or availability actually changes.

Every important spatially dependent interface must answer:

~~~math
\boxed{\text{WHERE?}}
\qquad
\boxed{\text{WHEN?}}
\qquad
\boxed{\text{HOW?}}
\qquad
\boxed{\text{HOW MUCH?}}
~~~

Spatial attributes include geography, network distance, route/accessibility, hazard exposure, co-location, feeder/road correspondence, service area and propagation path.


## 6. Effectiveness and efficiency are mandatory and distinct

~~~math
\boxed{
\Delta\mathfrak R
\neq
\mathcal E_{\rm fx}
\neq
\mathcal E_{\rm fy}
\neq
\Delta\mathbf J_{\rm sus}.
}
~~~

- Effectiveness asks whether the intended engineering/resilience target was achieved.
- Efficiency asks what resources were required for the achieved effect.
- Resilience asks how remain/return/confidence capability changed.
- Sustainability consequences ask what wider cross-sector effects occurred.

No future audit may collapse these quantities into one score without an independently justified aggregation rule.

## 8. Intervention versus transformation

~~~math
\boxed{
\text{intervention}
\neq
\text{transformation}.
}
~~~

An intervention can alter the current trajectory:

~~~math
a_k\rightarrow Y(t).
~~~

A transformation may alter the system that generates future trajectories:

~~~math
\boxed{
a_k
\rightarrow
\left(
\mathcal G,
\Theta,
\mathbb I,
\mathcal U_{\rm feas},
\mathcal A
\right)_{k+1}.
}
~~~

This creates path dependence:

~~~math
\boxed{
a_k
\rightarrow
\mathcal S_{k+1}
\rightarrow
\mathcal A_{k+1}.
}
~~~

A structural transformation claim is admissible only when the action actually changes architecture, capacity, technology, interface structure, feasible control/action sets or an equivalent long-lived configuration.

## 9. Adaptive engineering decision

Stage 7 retains feasibility-first decision logic and may evaluate sequences only where evidence, scope and preregistration justify them.

Every transformation/pathway claim must state:
- current system/configuration;
- intervention/action;
- state or structural transition;
- resulting feasible set;
- uncertainty domain;
- reversibility/lock-in assumptions;
- decision horizon;
- evidence/claim ceiling.

No deterministic pathway is assumed when uncertainty or future branching remains material.

## 10. Mandatory stage audit questions

### Stage 1 — Structure
What is located where? What is static, time-varying or mode-dependent? Which service needs attach to which entities/areas? Is structural possibility being confused with dependency?

### Stage 2 — Causality
What is the direction? What mechanism transfers influence? Where and when can it activate? What are delay, capacity, mode and uncertainty? Is common-cause exposure being confused with cascade?

### Stage 3 — Dynamics
Which accepted mechanisms are executable? What are operational/recovery timescales? Are flow/jump domains and directional interface states explicit? Does spatial or temporal aggregation erase the mechanism/QOI?

### Stage 4 — Knowledge and feasible action
What can be observed/estimated? What information/organizational/resource constraints restrict action? What is the decision delay? Does feasible control vary by location, time or mode?

### Stage 5 — Resilience
Can service remain acceptable? Can it return acceptably? Under what uncertainty/confidence? Which component/interface/pathway changes viability or recovery? Is diagnostic criticality separated from causal importance?

### Stage 6 — Intervention/performance/sustainability
What intervention acts where and when? What resilience change results? Was it effective? At what efficiency/resource use? What coupled-SoS sustainability consequences occur? Is burden shifting controlled? Are sustainability metrics kept distinct from requirements?

### Stage 7 — Decision/transformation
Does the action merely change a trajectory or transform future structure? What future feasible actions/configurations does it create/remove? Is sequencing/path dependence justified? Is the decision robust to uncertainty? Are preferences separate from evidence and feasibility?
