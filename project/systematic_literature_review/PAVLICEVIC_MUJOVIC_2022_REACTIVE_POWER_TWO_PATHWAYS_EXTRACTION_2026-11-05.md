## 1. Exact source problem and scientific distinction
EV station on-board chargers (AC Level 2 OBC scenario) may inject reactive power Q while consuming the same active battery charging power P, if converters are appropriately rated and a utility grid code permits it. Local reactive support can modify upstream voltage, current and apparent transformer loading. The paper evaluates:
- **GLOBAL distribution-network active energy loss**, summed over branches and 24 h, energy [kWh].
- **LOCAL distribution transformer insulation loss of life**, determined from thermal winding hot-spot HST and aging acceleration, dimensionless LOL fraction/percent or equivalent aging hours.
The objectives are distinct; reducing all-line losses need not minimize one particular transformer’s aging. This provides sustainability resource-efficiency proxy and long-term reliability/asset-life proxy, **not demonstrated full heatwave resilience or CO2 impact**.

## 2. Original method (follow sections 2–4)
- Network model: MATLAB, IEEE radial **33-bus** reference system, 12.66 kV base, residential/commercial/industrial time-scaled PQ loads. **Backward–forward sweep** power-flow solver. Initially identify EVCS placement by brute-force loss comparison; then enforce geographically distributed stations and compare Q operating settings.
- EV charging demand: Monte Carlo from probability models. Plug-in time **Beta mixture model** with separate weekday/weekend mixture parameters; daily travel distance **lognormal**, used to infer initial SOC and charging energy. BEV/PHEV proportions and charge powers are 2022 illustrative assumptions, not current universal EV fleet.
- Charger: AC Level 2 on-board converter; source assumes **5.3% converter apparent-power oversizing**, enabling Q_max approx **32.9% of its active charging power** without reducing active P in the idealized rating circle. This does NOT mean every real EVSE/OBC supports independent VAR dispatch; OBC, EVSE, and DC chargers must be distinguished.
- Original operation: a forecast-based set point for OBC power factor is chosen and **held constant for 24 hours**, so not online feedback or real-time tracking of measured transformer temperature.
- Physical chain: \(P_{TL}=P_L+P_{EVCS}\) [eq. 21], \(Q_{TL}=Q_L-Q_{EVCS}\) with injection positive [eq.22]; apparent kVA changes \(S=\sqrt{P^2+Q^2}\), changing current and load ratio. Winding HST = ambient + top-oil rise + winding HST over oil [eq.9], thermal transients [eq.10–16], aging acceleration factor [eq.17], equivalent aging [eq.18], percent insulation LOL [eq.19]. Original analysis assumes **constant ambient 30 °C** in the transformer thermal section; not a measured time-varying extreme-heat thermal benchmark.
- Pareto candidate solutions: reactive support scanned from \(0\) to \(Q_{max}\) in increments \(0.05Q_{max}\); **Grey Relational Analysis** (GRA, eq.23–31) used after nondominated trade-offs to rank two criteria. The paper says it primarily searched/evaluated alternative results rather than inventing a new optimization solver. **No proof of continuous/global optimum or model-predictive control.**

## 3. Exact bounded examples and numerical audit
- Base network: daily active demand reported 62,556–62,557 kWh (internal rounding mismatch); loss 2924 kWh, lowest bus voltage 0.9016 pu (18th hour at bus 18), according to §4.
- Placement case Table 4 at same added public station demand: **best** station positions [2,19,20,21] losses **3008 kWh/day**; **worst** [15,16,17,18] losses **4486 kWh/day**. This is a **test-system spatial placement sensitivity** not global field causal proof.
- §4.2 states approximately **3% daily loss reduction** from maximum reactive support compared to zero Q under study-specific station placement and network.
- Example transformer T1 = 200 kVA, increased EVCS demand 7%, workday Table 6: LOL **0.0202%** at Q=0, **0.0168%** at Q=(2/3)Qmax. Approx reduction **16.8%** in predicted LOL under same illustrative scenario; paper text describes about 17%; different reported intermediate '36.6 h' versus table may reflect numerical/rounding errors.
- T2 = 250 kVA, additional EVCS demand 40%, Pareto/GRA Table 8: **Q/Qmax = 0.80** ranked compromise with **3533.4 kWh/day** active losses, **24.012 equivalent aging h/day**. Q/Qmax=0.75 has **24.000 h/day** aging but 3540.0 kWh/day losses; Q/Qmax=1.00 has **3507.7 kWh/day** losses but **26.689 h/day** aging. This **demonstrates the noncoincident minima and trade-off** and that GRA choice is a subjective decision rule conditioned by weights.
- §4.3 Table 7 T2, 40% added penetration workday: LOL % Q=0: **0.0139%**, Q=2/3 Qmax: **0.0127%**, Q=Qmax: **0.0148%**. Greater Q is not always better for local aging.
- **Critical source data-quality issue:** §3 around Eq.19–20 states an assumed *normal insulation life* of '**18,000 h or 20.55 years**'; these values are inconsistent, because 20.55 years corresponds to **180,000 h**. Their 24h/180,000h=0.01333% calculation uses 180,000 h. Additional narrative inconsistencies/typos appear in §4.3, so independently verify published equations/denominator and reproduce from data before thesis quantitative adoption. Do not silently 'correct' author claims.

## 4. Equations: source attribution versus proposed thesis extension
**Source physical relations:** \(P_{TL}=P_L+P_{EV}\), \(Q_{TL}=Q_L-Q_{EV}\) with sign convention; \(T_H=T_a+\Delta T_{TO}+\Delta T_{H|TO}\); \(F_{AA}=\exp(15000/383-15000/(T_H+273))\) as adopted by 2022 paper. Requires actual equipment loading guide applicability, units and temperature-sensor semantics.
**Our extension, not quoted from source:** per-node/branch \(P,Q,V,I\) network equations; full verified physical HST dynamics \(\dot x_{tr}=f(x_{tr},I,T_a,u_{cool})\); EV charger converter limit \(p_{i,t}^2+q_{i,t}^2\le S_{i,max}^2\), with device support/availability; physically qualified heat-/power-dependent restrictions; EV battery SOC dynamics and unmet departure energy. Optimize on a small feasible Pareto frontier over \(E_{loss}\), \(L_{age}\), \(E_{unmet}\), with critical times/lag/temperature and optional price/true carbon (only if verified marginal emissions data). Under stochastic conditions demand distributions/joint uncertainty must be qualified. Never assert all EV chargers can supply reactive Q at full charging P without oversizing or compromise.
**Why Q can help:** For locally fixed active/net real P and voltage, reducing magnitude of net reactive consumption decreases approximate current \(I\propto\sqrt{P^2+Q_{net}^2}\), reducing I² thermal losses. **Excess injection** can produce negative net Q and increased apparent demand, changing local aging. Network-wide losses and a specific transformer can reach different minima due to branch placement/voltages. This is a mechanistic deduction, not a source-proven universal law for every feeder.
**SO1:** physical T→P path EV converter Q→ feeder flow/transformer load; P→T restriction still proposed/not demonstrated by this paper.
**SO2:** physically based coupled electric + oil/winding HST states plus stochastic EV requests, age metric, ODE/PDE or calibrated standard; the original primarily runs hourly/steady state power flows and aging transients with fixed ambient 30°C.
**SO3:** quantify predicted thermal-limit crossing, available charging kW, delivered kWh/departure unmet, recovery/delays under extreme heat; these require original additional physical constraints and mobility data and were NOT validated by source.

## 5. Resilience and sustainability evidence level
- Active losses and aging = **reliability and operational resource efficiency** proxies, not direct measured emissions, lifecycle impacts, environmental LCA or stress/recovery trajectories.
- Resilience under extreme heat requires **time-varying ambient hazard, transformer thermal inertia, threshold/derating/response, EV mobility services, recovery and uncertainty**. The source adopts 30°C fixed reference in §3 thermal analysis and is not a heat-wave field demonstration.
- Paper simulations not verified against a physically mapped, synchronized connected transformer + EVSE field thermal/control dataset. Existing project SINTEF transformer HST series cannot validate charger Q injection effect absent matched site harmonic/reactive-power streams and charger topology.
- A later **2025** paper Raghuveer, Bhalja & Agarwal DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsr.2024.111062 already proposes stochastic receding-horizon EMS coordinating EV active/reactive power, transformer aging and driving needs. This is an important novelty-boundary/falsification check before claiming first joint P/Q and aging scheduling.
