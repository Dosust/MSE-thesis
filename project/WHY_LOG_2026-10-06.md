# Why Log — 2026-10-06

Purpose: preserve the reasoning behind major thesis choices so humans and AI systems do not restart resolved debates.

## Decision W-001 — Thesis purpose

**Accepted**

Physics-based mathematical engineering is a **method** used to improve, adapt, and restore coupled electricity and mobility services under extreme heat.

**Rejected**

"Greater physical fidelity" as the thesis purpose.

**Why**

Model detail is only valuable if it changes a service prediction, engineering decision, or validated intervention outcome.

---

## Decision W-002 — Resilience action sequence

**Accepted / working-frozen**

```text
Improve → Adapt → Restore
```

**Why**

The sequence is useful as a thesis-defined engineering cycle and can be grounded in established preparedness/robustness, flexibility/adaptation, and recovery concepts.

**Boundary**

Do not claim it is the universal definition of resilience.

---

## Decision W-003 — Interdependency

**Accepted**

A P–T interdependency must expose:
- direction;
- physical/functional mechanism;
- state/exchange;
- service consequence;
- evidence.

**Rejected**

```text
co-location → dependency
```

**Why**

Common hazard exposure and functional dependency are different causal structures.

---

## Decision W-004 — Novelty

**Rejected as novelty claims**

- coupling Power and Transportation;
- EV charging in CPTNs;
- physics-based modeling by itself;
- resilience optimization by itself;
- Improve/Adapt/Restore wording by itself.

**Why**

Current literature already contains these elements in multiple forms.

**Open novelty boundary**

Whether a bounded, heat-dependent physical-state representation propagated through a qualified P–T electrification mechanism materially changes service trajectories and engineering actions.

This remains open until closest full-text studies are reviewed.

## Decision W-006 — Temperature-aware road-EV CPTN novelty boundary

**New evidence**

Li & Chang (2024) already model:

```text
ambient temperature
→ EV energy consumption
→ charging demand
→ charging-station load
```

inside a coupled transportation–distribution framework and apply dynamic charging pricing.

Ji et al. (2025) already report temperature-aware EV FCS selection/routing within a tri-level EV–FCS–PDN optimization framework.

**Therefore reject as novelty**

```text
temperature-aware EV charging-load modeling
temperature-aware FCS selection/routing
temperature-aware charging intervention
```

**Still open**

```text
explicit heat-dependent physical component states/capabilities
→ coupled electricity + mobility service trajectories
→ engineering consequence for Improve / Adapt / Restore
```

Do not promote this open distinction to a final gap until the remaining closest literature is reviewed.
