# Stage 6 Scientific Constitution and Handoff v1

## 1. Authority

Stage: 6 — Resilience Diagnosis, Engineering Intervention and Sustainability Consequences

RQ6: What sustainability requirements follow from resilience diagnosis and from the effectiveness, resource efficiency, and sustainability consequences of candidate interventions?

SO6: Derive evidence-qualified sustainability requirements from resilience diagnosis and intervention consequences, distinguishing effectiveness, resource efficiency, accessibility, energy, carbon and cost within declared accounting boundaries.

H4: Across the prespecified intervention and scenario set, engineering interventions that produce meaningful resilience improvement are not equivalent in their sustainability consequence vectors; at least two resilience-effective interventions produce materially different accessibility, energy, carbon, or cost consequences under the declared comparison rule.

This protocol preserves the canonical H4: consequence heterogeneity, not an assumption that resilience improvement is automatically sustainable.

## 5. Operational control versus strategic intervention

\[
\boxed{u(t)\neq a_k}
\]

Operational controls belong to Stages 4–5. Strategic intervention \(a_k\) changes infrastructure, capacity, interface structure, feasible action sets, information/organization, or declared requirements.

## 8. Permanent rules

### R1 — Resilience is not sustainability

\[
\boxed{
\Delta\mathfrak R>0
\not\Rightarrow
\Delta\mathbf J_{\rm sus}\text{ is favorable}
}
\]

Liu, Lu, and Faber (2024, DOI 10.1016/j.scs.2024.105647) and Sánchez-Silva et al. (2025, DOI 10.1016/j.strusafe.2024.102449) support treating resilience and sustainability as coupled but distinct.

### R2 — No single sustainability score by default

\[
\boxed{
\mathbf J_{\rm sus}
\neq
w^\top\mathbf J_{\rm sus}
}
\]

unless weights are independently justified. ISO/TS 14074:2022 treats weighting and single scores as value-based operations requiring explicit documentation.

### R3 — Common function and boundary

Alternatives are comparable only under the same \(FU_6\), lifecycle boundary, analysis horizon, scenario set, population/geography and service definition.

### R4 — Physics precedes sustainability arithmetic

Any intervention violating power/transport physics, feasibility, or upstream causal qualification is inadmissible regardless of a favorable cost/carbon result.

### R5 — No normative threshold from observed data alone

Observed consequence distributions can expose trade-offs but cannot create a hard requirement without external authority, stakeholder/value basis, or explicit research-assumption status.

### R6 — No social claim from aggregate service alone

Average accessibility or average power service cannot establish distributional equity.

### R7 — Carbon is not total environmental sustainability

ISO 14067 addresses climate change only.

### R8 — Two clocks

\[
\boxed{T_5\neq H_{LC}}
\]

\(T_5\) is the disruption/recovery horizon. \(H_{LC}\) is the lifecycle accounting horizon.

### R9 — Data do not govern the stage

\[
\boxed{
D_{\rm available}\rightarrow\mathcal R_6^{case},
\qquad
D_{\rm available}\not\rightarrow\mathcal C_6
}
\]
