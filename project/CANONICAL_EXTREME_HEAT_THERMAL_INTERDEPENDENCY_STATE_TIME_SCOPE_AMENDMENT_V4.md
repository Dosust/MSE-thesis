## 1. Active thesis focus

The thesis focuses only on **thermal interdependencies between the Power System and Electrified Transportation System and their consequences through physical state and time**.

Primary chain:

```text
Extreme Heat
→ Thermal Physical State
→ Thermal Capability / Constraint
→ Thermal Interdependency
→ State-Time Consequence
→ Electricity + Mobility Service Consequence
→ Resilience Interpretation
```

State and time remain the primary analytical dimensions.

## 1A. Meaning of state and time

State and time are governed by `CANONICAL_STATE_TIME_DEFINITIONS_V1.md`.

```text
State:
the minimum set of evolving physical, operational, energy, or degradation variables
required to characterize the current modeled condition and determine subsequent evolution.

Time:
the independent variable through which state evolution, critical transitions,
interdependency propagation, service consequences, and—where modeled—recovery
and degradation are ordered and quantified.
```

The thesis distinguishes:

```text
fast thermal state
≠ operational/energy state
≠ slow degradation/health state
≠ service
```

SOC is an energy state, not a thermal state. Aging can be a legitimate slow degradation state. External heat is forcing, not an internal state under the active convention. Capability and service are derived/response quantities rather than states unless a selected model explicitly gives them independent dynamics.

## 2. Physical core

```text
Power System P ↔ Electrified Transportation System T_e
```

The Power System and Electrified Transportation System remain distinct physical systems. Coupling/interface variables do not form a third infrastructure system.

## 3. Thermal-interdependency restriction

Only thermal interdependencies are active research mechanisms.

A thermal interdependency is a qualified directional dependency in which a heat- or temperature-dependent physical state, thermal capability, or thermally conditioned operating limit in one system, or in the power-transfer pathway, changes the physical or functional capability of the coupled system or changes electricity or mobility service over time.

Non-thermal Power–Transportation interdependencies remain outside the active analytical scope except as boundary conditions, controls, constraints, or supporting variables strictly required to represent a thermal mechanism.

## 4. Resilience interpretation

> **Resilience is the ability of the coupled Power and Electrified Transportation systems to maintain or recover required electricity and mobility services as thermal states and thermal interdependencies evolve over time.**

Resilience is evaluated at the service level through time.

Permanent distinction:

```text
Thermal State ≠ Capability ≠ Service ≠ Resilience
```

## 5. Two-pathway sustainability assessment

Sustainability is now a **bounded downstream assessment** of the thermal/service consequences and engineering responses. It does not broaden the thesis into generic sustainability modeling.

Canonical definition:

> **Sustainability in this thesis is assessed through two pathways: (1) the direct environmental, economic, and social consequences of thermal interdependencies on electricity and mobility services, and (2) the environmental, economic, and social consequences of engineering responses used to improve, adapt, or restore those services. Both pathways are evaluated within a declared system boundary and time horizon.**

### Pathway 1 — Direct sustainability consequence

```text
Extreme Heat
→ Thermal Interdependency
→ Electricity + Mobility Service Consequence
→ Environmental + Economic + Social Consequence
```

### Pathway 2 — Engineering-response sustainability consequence

```text
Improve / Adapt / Restore
→ Changed State / Capability / Service
→ Environmental + Economic + Social Consequence
```

These are two pathways within one sustainability framework.

## Canonical state-time clarification — 2026-10-08

### Canonical meaning of state and time

Use `CANONICAL_STATE_TIME_DEFINITIONS_V1.md` for all active interpretation of state and time.

> **State in this thesis refers to the minimum set of evolving physical or operational variables required to characterize the current condition of the Power System and Electrified Transportation System and to determine their subsequent evolution under extreme-heat forcing. The primary states of interest are thermal states; non-thermal states are included only where required to represent the thermal interdependency or its service consequence.**

> **Time in this thesis represents the temporal evolution of those states and the timing of critical thermal transitions, interdependency propagation, service degradation, and, where modeled, service recovery.**

The controlling chain is:

```text
Extreme Heat
→ Thermal State Evolution
→ Thermal Capability / Constraint
→ Qualified Thermal Interdependency
→ State-Time Consequence
→ Electricity / Mobility Service Consequence
```

Preserve:

```text
external forcing ≠ system state
state ≠ capability ≠ service
thermal state ≠ operational/energy state
transition time ≠ propagation delay ≠ service-consequence time ≠ recovery time
```

This clarification does not freeze the exact active state variables or time horizon; those remain OPEN until independently qualified.
