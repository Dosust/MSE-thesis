# Integration-First Thesis Approach — Working Note V1

**Status:** WORKING RESEARCH STRATEGY — NOT CANONICAL, NOT A NOVELTY CLAIM  
**Date:** 2026-10-08  
**Thesis:** Physics-Based Mathematical Engineering of Power–Transportation Interdependencies for Resilience

## Purpose
Use verified, existing knowledge to assemble and test a *bounded* engineering explanation of how extreme-heat-conditioned thermal states and cross-system mechanisms propagate through time to required electricity and mobility services. The contribution should come from an evidence-qualified integration, testable implementation, and clarity about what the combined model adds. **Do not begin by claiming no one has ever done it or by seeking a large revolutionary gap.**

## What counts as a defensible integration contribution?
A specific, bounded Power–Electrified Transportation case in which:
1. the relevant existing component models, observations and operating constraints are traced to their sources;
2. at least one direction of thermal interdependency is physically qualified rather than inferred from shared extreme heat or mere electrical connection;
3. the assembled state-time representation is verified and evaluated against independent evidence where available;
4. justified critical transitions, propagation times and **both electricity and mobility-service consequences** are quantified within explicit validity limits;
5. the combined analysis is shown, using a physically consistent comparison, to answer a service-relevant question that isolated analyses do not answer as adequately **for this selected case**;
6. resilience and the two sustainability pathways are interpreted within available evidence; criticality and engineering responses are supporting analyses, not new primary objectives.

A literature assembly alone is **not** enough. The original work is the justified formulation, coupling qualification, case-specific test, computation, uncertainty interpretation and engineering conclusion. The size of the contribution is determined by results and evidence; it cannot be promised in advance.

## Practical workflow
**Evidence inventory** -> **mapping of supported vs unqualified connections** -> **select one feasible physical application** -> **qualify directional thermal coupling** -> **admit minimum sufficient states** -> **build and test physics-based state-time model** -> **measure service consequences** -> **evaluate uncertainty** -> **supporting resilience/response/sustainability interpretation** -> **state supported contribution and limitations**.

## Scientific claims that are NOT acceptable
- "Nobody has studied this" without comprehensive, appropriately bounded prior-art evidence.
- "First ever" or "revolutionary" as motivational phrasing.
- "Heat impacts both systems; therefore a thermal interdependency exists."
- "Combining two published equations validates the combined model."
- "Simulation proves causality" or "AI confirms the mechanism."
- "A better temperature estimate necessarily means better electricity/mobility resilience."
- "More components or a more complicated model implies stronger science."

## Thesis presentation / paper phrasing
Prefer: "This work investigates whether integrating [specific established component mechanisms] within [declared case and boundary] improves the explanation or quantification of [specific time-dependent electricity/mobility service consequences]. Its contribution and validity will be determined through evidence qualification, matched comparisons and validation."

Avoid promising results or asserting novelty before a documented review and completed analysis.

## Spring 2027 discipline
Use existing validated building blocks where transferability can be justified; do not attempt to derive every component from first principles. Resolve equipment/data feasibility first and preserve time for verification, independent validation, writing, committee review and the Spring defense.
